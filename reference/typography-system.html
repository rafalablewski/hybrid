01 — The two faces

Three cuts, and a rule that picks between them.

The decision procedure is one sentence long, and it is why the app can be read at arm's length: monospaced texture means "this is a reading from the world" before a single glyph is identified.

Söhne

Language

Every word a person wrote — titles, prose, labels, controls. Four weights on one skeleton, so a weight is a stem and nothing else.

Söhne Mono

Measurement

Every value a machine measured, and every uppercase eyebrow. 0.600em advance on every glyph, which is what actually holds a column together.

ITC Garamond

Interpretation

One element per screen. Never a figure, a control, a label or a state. Never below 24dp. English only.

The test for a borderline case is not "did a machine produce it" — a coach's note is typed and a tempo is prescribed. It is: is this a value with a fixed format that a person parses positionally? 3-1-1-0, 5:12, RPE 8 and 24 × 8 all pass. A sentence containing a number does not, and its number stays in Söhne, proportional — a figure in prose is prose.

02 — What was wrong

The system was built for a different pair of fonts.

Söhne, Söhne Mono and ITC Garamond were swapped in underneath a system designed around a different pair of faces. The swap moved the faces and left every optical constant where it was. Four consequences, each invisible on any single screen:

  • TrackingThe bands belonged to the old face. They were adopted with a note that their values were chosen so the the previous face call sites would not move. Söhne is fitted tighter — n carries 0.144em of sidebearing against its ~0.17 — so that tightening on top of Söhne's own tighter fit over-tightened every heading in the app.
  • WeightThe heaviest cut was the default. Dreiviertelfett (0.16em stem) carried 298 call sites against 258 for the regular and 81 for the medium — the distribution inverted — with 62 of them at reading size or below, where a 2.2dp stroke closes the counters of a, e and s.
  • LeadingA figure's line box was a round number. flush was 1.00 — the value nearest the intent. The mono figure set spans 0.804em, so every hero figure carried 9dp of box nothing could occupy.
  • PairingThe serif's x-height was a stale literal. 0.445 in the source against 0.4409 in the binary, and its leading ratio multiplied an em the size compensation had already inflated by 18.6% — so a display-size pull quote was set at body leading.
None of these looks broken in isolation. That is the whole difficulty, and it is why every constant now resolves through theme/face-metrics.ts, whose figures are re-read from the shipped binaries by a test that fails the build when they drift.

03 — The ladder

One ratio. One reference size. Every rung derived.

The ratio is a major third, 1.25, taken in half-steps of √1.25 = 1.118034. Every rung is 16 × STEP^n, so the ladder is one ratio throughout and a proposed size either has an exponent or it does not.

The golden ratio was measured and rejected: φ from a 16dp body puts the next rung at 25.9 and the one after at 41.9, which cannot furnish an interface — a UI needs a rung between a list row and a section title, and φ has none to give.

16dp seeds it, and the seed is measured rather than picked: it is the size Söhne is fitted for, the one size on the ladder whose drawn sidebearings need no correction at all.

It does a second job. The tracking curve measures its correction as a distance from this same 16, so the type scale and the optical correction share one origin. One number, two axes, and they cannot drift apart.

Irradiation runs the other way on a lit surface: dark ink on a bright ground reads lighter than it measures. weightOnGround() steps the same ladder one rung up rather than forking a second one, and takeover — the Wrapped's full-bleed covers — is its one consumer.

07 — Tracking

A continuous curve, zero at the reference size.

Tracking is a function of optical size, not of what the caller thinks the text is for:

track(size) = 0.018938 × ln(16 / size)

Zero at 16 by construction — that is where Söhne's drawn sidebearings are already right, so the correction there must be nothing. Above it the curve tightens, because large type carries too much air at sidebearings drawn for reading sizes. Below it the curve opens up, because small type loses counter definition without a trace of air put back.

Logarithmic, because optical size is perceived in ratios — exactly like the ladder itself. The correction from 16 to 32 has to equal the correction from 32 to 64, and only a log does that. A linear ramp runs away at the top and does nothing at the bottom.

What replaced a step table

The band table had two defects beyond carrying the previous face's numbers. It had steps: 25dp returned −0.015em and 26dp −0.020em, so a rung boundary was a visible discontinuity in the middle of the ladder — and a computed size could land either side of one for reasons having nothing to do with optics.

Every move is a loosening. The reading band does not move at all, the display band gets its air back, and the top of the ladder lands exactly where it already was. That is the shape you would predict if the old values had been fitted to a wider face — which is what the source said they were.

All-caps versus sentence case

  • CapsMono only, and only at 10–11dp. Uppercase is a structural signal in this system — it says "this is a division of the page", never "this is important". Allowing it at reading sizes is how an app grows shouting section titles.
  • SentenceEverything else, without exception. Headings, buttons, labels, chips, empty states. A section head is a bold display-face title in chalk with its meta on the right of the same row — never a marker on the left.
  • NeverTitle Case On Interface Copy. It reads as a product from 2009 and it makes German and Polish strings unreadable.

08 — Figures

The mono cut is the mechanism, not a preference.

tabular-nums does nothing on these binaries. The shipped Söhne cuts carry no OpenType features at all — GSUB is empty in all seven files — so there is no tnum to activate, and the sans digits stay proportional across eight distinct advances. What actually holds a column together is the mono cut's uniform 0.600em advance. That is why every measured value must be mono: the rule is load-bearing, not stylistic.

Two failures a proportional figure causes

  • ColumnsFour stat tiles align on their glyphs rather than their digit slots, so 111 and 888 are visibly different widths and nothing lines up under anything.
  • RollsA rolling figure gives each digit its own column and animates only the ones that changed — so a units digit going 1 → 8 resizes its column mid-animation and shoves every digit beside it sideways. The roll exists to make one change read as one event; a proportional numeral makes the whole figure twitch instead.

A unit is derived from its figure

The figure is mono; the unit is not. A measured value is the subject and kg is a caption attached to it, so they differ in face, weight, size and ink — otherwise 92.4kg reads as one seven-character token and the figure stops being scannable.

Unit size is 0.42 × the figure, clamped between 11 and 18: below figureSm a unit would fall under the label floor, and at stat an unclamped unit would reach heading size. Gap is 0.25em — except % ° ′ ″, which bind tight, because those are modifiers of the figure rather than units of it.

09 — The serif pairing

Two faces read as one size when their x-heights match.

Not when their point sizes do. Lowercase is what a reader measures a face by; the em square is an arbitrary box neither face fills. So the serif's rung is derived, never typed:

fs.editorial = fs.display × (0.523 / 0.4409)
  = 28 × 1.18621 = 33.21 → 33

Move display and the serif follows it. A hard-coded value would have gone on claiming a match it no longer had, and the failure is invisible — two faces at slightly different optical sizes just look faintly wrong.

It matches on caps as well. Garamond's short caps (0.623em) and Söhne's tall ones (0.718em) very nearly cancel the size compensation: at 28 / 33 the two cap heights are 20.1 and 20.56 — half a dp apart on a 20dp cap. That coincidence is most of why these two faces can share a screen at all.

Why ITC Garamond, and not a real Garamond

ITC Garamond is a 1975 phototype interpretation, not an old-style revival, and its x-height is famously enormous for a Garamond: 0.441em against a true Garamond's ~0.40. That is exactly what lets it sit beside a grotesque without reading as a smaller, older voice — it needs 17.6% compensation where a true old-style would need 31% and still read as another era.

The leading defect it caused

A leading ratio multiplies the em — and the serif's em is 18.6% bigger than the size it is pretending to be. So setting the editorial voice at snug gave it 43dp of line box, which is 1.53× its apparent size: body leading, on a display-size pull quote. Derived from the face's own 0.9209em ink span instead, it is 1.23 — 41dp, or 1.45× apparent.

The rules, and they are ratcheted in the test suite

  • OneOne element per screen. A second means neither is the conclusion.
  • NeverNever on a screen used during training. Mid-set the athlete needs measurement, and a serif sentence is not measurement. Workout, interval timer and the active endurance screens are closed to it.
  • NeverNever a figure, unit, control, label, badge or state. Never uppercase, never letterspaced positive, never in the accent colour — the accent means go, and a conclusion is not a destination.
  • FloorNever below 24dp. Garamond's joins break up on the near-black ground under that.
  • LanguageEnglish only. The binary carries Ł ł ó but not ą ę ś ż ź ć ń, so a Polish or Czech string cannot be set in it at all. Every consumer falls back to the sans rather than rendering a line with holes in it.
  • PairingNever mix the two faces in one line. The attribution under a quote is sans — a serif source line reads as one continuous piece of setting, so it competes with the sentence instead of receding from it. The face change is the demotion.

11 — Fitness surfaces

What a training app asks of type that a website does not.

Three conditions shape every decision above: the screen is read at arm's length, by someone out of breath, often mid-movement. That is why the reading floor moved up, why figures get their own cut, and why the accent is spent so carefully.

The metric display

  • OneOne hero figure per screen. A second means neither is the answer. Everything else steps down to figure or below.
  • FitA mono figure's width is characters × size × 0.600 before it is drawn, so a layout can ask whether a figure fits before committing to a size. The activity card shipped four figures in quarter-width columns and two broke mid-word; the arithmetic that would have caught it is one multiplication.
  • CeilingNothing above stat is type. The Wrapped's count-up (76dp) is not inside a page — it is the panel — and a watermark glyph bled off a card corner carries no reading role at all.
  • ClockA live clock is zero-padded (01:42:18) so it never changes width crossing the hour; a finished one reads as prose (1:42:18).

Where the accent may go

Wild Lime is the one action accent, and it means go. It is not an emphasis colour and not a "good news" colour. A record, a total, a completed session — those are facts, and they are set in chalk.

  • YesA control that leads somewhere. A kicker naming a live state. The lit end of a comparison row.
  • NoThe editorial sentence — a conclusion is not a destination. An expander's count or label — an expander never goes anywhere, so it is ash. A hero figure — the number is the subject, and colouring it says the number is a link.
Colour is never the only channel. Chartreuse against terracotta reads as one grey to a red-green athlete, so every toned figure carries its direction in the type too — a signed percentage, a rail, a word.

12 — Dark & light

The same ladder, one rung apart.

Light ink on a dark ground irradiates: the lit strokes bleed outward and every weight reads heavier than it measures. Dark ink on a lit ground does the reverse. So a style tuned on one ground is wrong on the other by about one weight step — and the correction is exactly that, rather than a second ladder that would drift from the first.

Tracking

Unchanged. Irradiation thickens strokes, not sidebearings — the counters close before the fit does, so tracking is the wrong lever and reaching for it here would loosen text that is not too tight.

Ink

Primary is Stalactite #f7f6f3, not pure white: on near-black, #fff blooms enough to soften the counters at reading size. Secondary is Slate Gray #8a9691 — a green-grey, chosen rather than sampled off the ground.

14 — Implementation

Where each thing lives.

  • Metricspackages/core/src/theme/face-metrics.ts — the only file allowed to state a font measurement. Re-read from the binaries by its own test.
  • Scalepackages/core/src/scale.ts — the ladder, leading, the tracking curve, spacing, the measure.
  • Stylespackages/core/src/theme/typography.ts — the named styles as data: a cut, a weight, a rung, a leading role, a tracking role, an ink role.
  • Renderapps/mobile/lib/ui.tsx — ty(palette, token) turns a style into a React Native TextStyle. Web reads the same tokens through CSS custom properties.
A style holds role names, never numbers. That is what makes a face swap two constants instead of 2,274 call sites — and it is the only reason this rebuild could move eight rungs of the ladder at once without touching a screen.

The five rules a reviewer can check

  • 01Every size is rung(n) for an integer n. There is nowhere to put a number that felt right.
  • 02Every measured value is mono and tabular. Every word a person wrote is sans.
  • 03On ink, nothing above 600 — in either cut.
  • 04Uppercase is mono, at 10–11dp, and nowhere else.
  • 05One serif element per screen, never during training, never a figure, never below 24dp, English only.

Specimens on this page are set in Libre Franklin, IBM Plex Mono and Newsreader — stand-ins chosen for the right category, not the right drawing. Söhne and ITC Garamond are licensed and must not be served from a public page, which is the same rule the repository's own guard enforces. The numbers are the specification; the letterforms here are an illustration of it.
