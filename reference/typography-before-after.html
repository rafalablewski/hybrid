03 — Tracking

Archivo’s step table, replaced by Söhne’s curve.

The bands were adopted with a note saying their em figures were chosen so the Archivo call sites would not move. Söhne is fitted tighter than Archivo — n carries 0.144em of sidebearing against roughly 0.17 — so Archivo’s tightening on top of Söhne’s own tighter fit over-tightened every heading in the app. Every change below is a loosening, which is exactly the shape you would predict if the old values had been fitted to a wider face.

05 — The figure line box

1.00 was a round number. 0.804em is the ink.

A figure has no second line and no descender past the cap band, so a title’s leading buys line box that nothing can ever occupy. flush was added to fix that and stopped at 1.00 — the round number nearest the intent. Why the leading was wrong, precisely

The serif’s size is inflated 18.6% so its x-height lands where the sans’s does. A leading ratio multiplies that inflated em — so setting the editorial voice at snug gave it 1.53× its apparent size. That is body leading, on a display-size pull quote: precisely the “reads as a caption for something else” complaint the token was created to fix, arriving back through the other axis.

lh.editorial is derived from ITC Garamond’s own ink span — 0.9209em, ascender to descender — times 4/3, the oldest rule in setting display type. 1.23, or 1.47× apparent.

11 — What did not change

And the two things worth arguing about.

Left standing, deliberately

  • Spacing0 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 / 12 / 16 / 20 / 24 / 32 / 40. Already a 4dp grid with two named 2dp half-rungs for dense internals. The brief asked for a spacing scale derived from the same modular system; this one already answers to a baseline grid, and regenerating it would have moved every gap in the app to fix nothing.
  • CutsThree, and the rule that picks between them. Sans for what a person wrote, mono for what a machine measured, serif for the one sentence that concludes something. The rule was right; only the numbers under it were wrong.
  • InkStalactite and Slate Gray, primary and secondary. A style names the ROLE and the renderer resolves it, because the same style is used on the page ground and on a card.
  • Mono0.600em advance. The constant did not move across the face swap — and a test now says that was luck rather than design, because the next face may not share it.
  • FiguresSix figure rungs, the unit ratio, the tight-bound set, U+00D7. The readout weight distinction — a clock is 500, a result is 600 — survives untouched. It is the system’s one semantic weight rule and it was already correct.

The two things to push back on

The reading text got bigger. body 13→14, bodyLg 14→16, and eight rungs moved in total — roughly 10% more type for every layout that was tuned by eye to the old ones. I think it is right for an app read at arm’s length mid-set, and a real modular scale seeded at the face’s own design size demands it. But the tests and the bundle export cannot see a card that now overflows, and I have not seen the app render. That is the claim that wants your eyes.
F.black collapsed onto F.bold. Both resolve to Halbfett now, which is the rule — on ink there is no weight above 600 — but any site leaning on the step between them has lost it. I stopped there rather than sweeping 298 call sites: deciding per site whether the emphasis was ever wanted needs a rendered proof, not a grep. It is recorded as weight-ladder-migration, planned.

Every optical constant now resolves through packages/core/src/theme/face-metrics.ts, whose own test re-reads the figures out of the shipped font binaries — OS/2 for the CFF Söhne cuts, glyf bounding boxes for the TrueType serif — so a font swap fails the build rather than quietly invalidating the system built on it.

pnpm --filter @hybrid/core test4,193 pass
pnpm --filter @hybrid/mobile test305 pass — this is the one carrying the design-token ratchets
npx expo export --platform iosbundles

Specimens are set in Libre Franklin, IBM Plex Mono and Newsreader — stand-ins in the right categories. Söhne and ITC Garamond are licensed and must not be served from a public page, which is the rule the repository’s own guard already enforces against globals.css. Sizes, weights, leading and tracking on this page are exact; the letterforms are an illustration of them.
