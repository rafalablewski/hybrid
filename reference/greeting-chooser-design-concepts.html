HYBRID — Today screen, first-run state

Greeting → chooser: 8 concepts

The screenshot shows the first-run Today screen. Between “Good afternoon, Rafal” and “How do you want to start?” the page loses its thread — this file diagnoses why, then shows eight ways to fix it, each one rendered in the real Aurora tokens.

The diagnosis

  • Two competing headlines. The greeting (22, bold) and the chooser question (18, extra-bold) are nearly the same size and the same weight, stacked four lines apart. Neither wins, so the eye bounces.
  • Three type voices in four lines. Bold display greeting, mono date, bold display question, mono “FREE” — the ramp never steps down cleanly; it alternates.
  • Both lines address you. The greeting speaks (“Good afternoon”), then the question speaks again (“How do you want to…”). Two openings to one conversation.
  • “FREE” floats. It hangs off the question’s baseline with no relationship to anything — it reads as a stray label, not a fact about the three cards below.

The Ive lens for everything below: Simplicity is not the absence of clutter — it is describing the purpose of an object in the most efficient way. One screen, one voice, one hierarchy. When a label only narrates what the eye already sees, delete it; when two elements say one thing, fuse them.

01 — FUSE THE TWO HEADLINES

One Question

The greeting and the question merge into a single headline: “How do you want to start, Rafal?” The time-of-day and date demote to one mono kicker line, with “FREE” finally anchored on the same line — it now describes the zone it sits above. Four lines become three; two headlines become one.

The Ive moveFusion. Two elements that address the same person in the same breath are one element. The warmth survives — the name simply moves into the question.

02 — DELETE THE SECOND HEADLINE

Nothing to Narrate

The greeting is the screen’s only headline — promoted to 28 so it clearly owns the page. The chooser header is deleted entirely: three cards that each say Follow a plan / Build your own / Just train do not need a sentence announcing that a choice is available. “FREE” slides onto the date line, where meta already lives.

The Ive moveDeference. The interface shouldn’t narrate what the athlete can already see — the same principle the plan card in this codebase already follows (“no kicker or eyebrow: the plan names itself”). Apply it one card earlier.

03 — KEEP BOTH, ENFORCE A RAMP

The Ladder

Both lines survive, but on a strict typographic ladder that only ever steps down: mono eyebrow date → 26 greeting → hairline → 14 zone label → cards. The question stops competing because it drops two full sizes and sits behind a rule that marks the zone change. This is the least invasive fix — pure scale discipline, no copy changes.

The Ive moveOrder. When everything is bold, nothing is. A hierarchy you can feel without reading is the cheapest kind of calm.

04 — ONE OBJECT, ONE MATERIAL

One Surface

Greeting, date, question and options fuse into a single card — one object the athlete reads top to bottom. The question shrinks to a mono zone label inside the surface, and the three options become hairline-divided rows instead of three floating boxes. The page goes from five separate objects to one.

The Ive moveUnity of material. Related things share one enclosure; the enclosure itself communicates the relationship, so no header has to.

05 — ONE VOICE, TWO SENTENCES

Dialogue

If both lines must speak, let them speak in one voice: the greeting and the question become two sentences of a single serif utterance — same face, same size, the question stepping back into ash italic. The date retires to the header, under the wordmark. Below, the options shed their boxes entirely and become a quiet hairline list — the words carry everything.

The Ive moveWarmth through restraint. The most human treatment isn’t a bigger greeting — it’s the app speaking like a person, once, and then getting out of the way.

06 — ONE HEADLINE, ONE SLAB

Monolith

The greeting stands alone in generous air — the only headline. The three options fuse into a single machined slab: one radius, equal rows, thin dividers, colour confined to the glyphs, a chevron promising depth. “FREE” is engraved once into the slab’s footer, where it describes exactly the object it belongs to. No section header exists because the slab is the section.

The Ive moveHonest material. Three choices of equal rank should look like one instrument with three positions, not three products on a shelf.

07 — SAY IT WITH LIGHT, NOT WORDS

Time as Material

“Good afternoon” stops being only a string — the aurora field itself warms with the hour (sand at midday, terracotta at dusk, teal at dawn), so the time-of-day is felt before it’s read. With atmosphere carrying the mood, the copy compresses hard: one meta line, no question, and the three options tighten into a compact tile row — titles and verbs only, subtitles cut.

The Ive moveLet the material do the talking. Colour and light communicate faster than a sentence, and a screen that needs fewer words was designed better.

08 — THE DEEPEST CUT

The Question Is the Greeting

Delete “Good afternoon” altogether. One sentence carries the name, the warmth and the purpose: “Rafal, how do you want to start?” — the name set in chartreuse so the personal note survives the cut. The date tucks under the wordmark; the cards shed their subtitles and become title-plus-verb rows, because a first-run user decides on the titles alone. This is the fewest elements any of the eight concepts uses.

The Ive moveRuthless subtraction. Keep removing until removing one more thing would break it — the greeting was ceremony; the question, with your name in it, is the product.
