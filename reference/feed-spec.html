HYBRID — product design specification — v1.0 — August 2026 — CONNECT

The feed where a number is worth something

A complete specification for HYBRID's social feed: information architecture, feed anatomy, 34 card types, interaction design, ranking, engagement loops, the visual system, ten wireframes, and a critical self-review. Built to be handed to product, design, engineering, and investors as one blueprint.

Grounded in the shipped codebase: packages/core/src/social.ts (feed engine, kudos, relations), attestation.ts (Verified Strength Record), the Aurora design system (theme/tokens.ts, scale.ts, nav-bar.ts), and the production schema (prisma/schema.prisma). Everything proposed here is written against what exists — each section closes with what is net-new versus already shipped.

Thesis

00

Every strength number on the internet is self-reported, and therefore worthless. There is no strength equivalent of a verified race time. HYBRID already built the ladder that fixes this — the Verified Strength Record — and the feed is where that ladder becomes a social object.

Strava's feed works because a run is hard to fake: GPS, pace, heart rate arrive as evidence, not claims. Lifting feeds — Hevy's above all — are weak because every number in them is typed. The result is a feed of receipts nobody can trust and therefore nobody discusses. Our wedge is that HYBRID posts carry proof: the tier badge (claimed, sensed, witnessed, recorded, instrumented, sanctioned) travels with every figure, the watch's recording outranks the keyboard everywhere (the device-truth rule, already law in this codebase), and the co-sign flow makes verification itself a social act — every witness request is also an invite.

The second structural bet: the workout is the post. Strava solved cold-start content by making posting a byproduct of living. We inherit that — a finished session, a PR, a recap is already a fully-formed card before the athlete types a word. The composer exists, but it is the garnish, not the meal.

The third: this feed lives in the locker room, not the town square. It is a tab inside Today, one swipe from the athlete's own dashboard — training context surrounds it. It is not an app-within-an-app competing with the logger for identity.

Before designing — the competitive read

01

What makes X addictive

  • Speed as a feature. The timeline paints instantly, actions are optimistic, nothing blocks on the network. The cost of one more flick is zero, so the flick never stops.
  • Variable reward at high frequency. Short posts mean high cadence; every refresh is a slot-machine pull with a real chance of something excellent.
  • The reply is the content. X's unit of value is not the post but the argument under it. Conversation compounds: each reply is a new post with its own audience.
  • Zero-friction authoring. The compose box is one tap away and a post is one sentence. The gap between thought and publication is seconds.
  • Live events. When something happens in the world, X is where it happens first. Recency is a ranking feature and a habit anchor.
  • Status made legible. Follower counts, view counts, ratios — metrics turn posting into a game with a visible score. (This is also X's poison: the game selects for outrage.)

What makes Strava addictive

  • The activity is the post. You produce content by training. No creative act, no blank-page anxiety, no cold-start problem. The feed fills itself.
  • Kudos, not likes. A bounded, warm, one-tap gesture with no counter-culture of ratios. Giving kudos is cheap; receiving them after a hard session lands emotionally.
  • Evidence, not claims. The map, the pace chart, the heart-rate trace. Numbers arrive with their own proof, which is why comparing them is meaningful.
  • Asynchronous competition. Segments and leaderboards let you race people who aren't there. Yesterday's you, your club, a stranger on the same hill.
  • Aggregates as identity. Weekly stats, annual totals, the year-in-sport. Strava tells you who you are becoming, and people return to watch that number grow.
  • Clubs as accountability. A small group seeing whether you showed up is a stronger retention force than any notification.

Why Hevy's social layer is weak

  • Every card is the same receipt. A 200 kg PR and a Tuesday accessory day render identically: exercise list, sets, volume. The feed has no concept of a moment, so nothing stands out and nothing is worth reacting to.
  • Numbers without proof. All figures are typed. Impressive numbers read as noise or lies; honest numbers get no credit. There is nothing to discuss because there is nothing to believe.
  • Likes without conversation. Comments exist but the content gives them nothing to grab. No questions, no form clips, no programs, no controversy — receipts don't start arguments.
  • No graph tools. Discovery is near-nonexistent: no gym graph, no strength-peer matching, no coach layer. Your feed is the three friends you invited manually.
  • Nothing on rest days. The feed only speaks when someone trains. Four days a week it has no reason to be opened — precisely the days a social product must fill.

Opportunities nobody has solved

Verified strength

No product can answer "did that lift happen?" HYBRID's attestation ladder (tiers 0–2 shipped) makes the feed the first place a strength number carries provenance — and the public read API later makes it the institutional record.

The two-person primitive

Lifting structurally requires a second person and no software serves that relationship. The Spotter Protocol (planned) turns spotting into pairing, co-signing, credited reps and a feed card — and every use is an invite.

Rest-day content

Recovery, readiness, streaks, recaps, questions, polls, research digests — states worth sharing that don't require a barbell. A feed for seven days a week, not three.

Programs ranked by outcome

Program selection is the highest-stakes decision a lifter makes, made on influencer vibes. The Program Efficacy Index (shipped) turns "this program, run by people like you, produced this" into shareable feed content nobody else has.

The coach as a citizen, not an ad

Coaches are the highest-value posters in fitness and every platform treats them as spam. A coach layer with real storefronts (shipped) plus feed-native advice and client-win cards makes coaching content welcome.

A feed that knows a PR from a Tuesday

Rank the moment, not the post. A milestone should interrupt; an ordinary session should aggregate. No fitness feed does this; it is the single largest quality lever available.

Design principles

02

Every decision downstream of this point must be defensible against these eight. When two conflict, the earlier one wins.

1 — Earn the number

Verification is the spine. Tier badges travel with every figure, on every surface. Verified figures render at full weight; claimed figures render quieter. Never shame the unverified — provenance is metadata, not a caste system.

2 — The workout is the post

Zero-authoring default. Finishing a session mints a complete, beautiful card; sharing it is one decision, not a creative project. Typed posts exist but the system never depends on them.

3 — Rank moments, not posts

A first pull-up outranks a follower count. Moment weight (PR, milestone, comeback, streak) is the first term of the ranking function, ahead of engagement probability — see D6.

4 — Kudos, not clout

Bounded, warm metrics. No follower counts on cards, no view counts, no ratios. The score of this game is training consistency, not audience size.

5 — The device outranks the keyboard

Already law in this codebase (device-truth rule): measured figures beat typed ones everywhere — durations, distances, paces, heart rate. The feed reads through deviceTrueSession() like every other surface.

6 — Dense, with hierarchy

Strength athletes read numbers fluently; don't hide them. Linear's clarity and Notion's spacing: mono tabular data under bold display headlines, one accent per card, air between zones.

7 — Nothing performative by default

Private-by-schema stays private: Session.note, mood, feel and fatigue are owner-only and never serialised to the feed (already enforced). Sharing granularity is chosen at share time, not discovered after.

8 — Every card points back to the bar

The feed's job is to make you train, not to make you scroll. Cards carry training-native actions: match this lift, try this program, join this challenge, schedule this session. Session-quality north star, not time-on-feed.

Design consequence

Beginner and elite must coexist in one feed. Aspiration is a spice, not the diet.

Deliverable 1 — Information architecture

D1

Where the feed lives

HYBRID's bottom nav is fixed at five tabs — Today, Nutrition, Train, More, Profile (nav-bar.ts) — and Today carries three pills: Dashboard, Performance, Feed (today-tabs.ts). We keep this. The feed does not get a sixth tab, for three deliberate reasons: the detached nav slot is reserved for Apple's search role; a separate social tab reframes HYBRID as a social app it isn't; and adjacency to Dashboard means every feed visit starts one swipe from the athlete's own data — the comparison that makes fitness feeds sticky.

Feed hierarchy

  • For you — the ranked feed (D6). Default tab. Interleaves followed content with peer-band, gym, club and coach content, all labelled with a "why you're seeing this" affordance.
  • Following — strictly chronological, strictly people you follow. Non-negotiable escape hatch; it keeps the ranked feed honest and the trust intact. Remembers your last-read position.
  • Clubs — the feeds of clubs you've joined (planned capability social-leaderboard-clubs), merged, with per-club filter pills. Until clubs ship, this tab is hidden — never an empty room.

Discovery hierarchy

  • Search — one field, four result sections: People (handle + real name — an email is never shown, per the shipped identity rule), Gyms, Clubs, Exercises (an exercise page shows its verified leaderboard + recent public sets).
  • Suggestions — existing /api/social/suggestions, upgraded with graph reasons: same gym, mutual friends, same program, same strength band, contacts (opt-in).
  • Coach directory — the shipped marketplace (components/coaches.tsx): verified-first directory → storefront → one-tap enrolment.
  • Leaderboard — friends-only today (shipped), clubs and challenges when social-leaderboard-clubs lands.

Profile hierarchy

Top to bottom: identity (avatar, real name, handle, gym, coach/elite chips) → follow/message state → showcase (the athlete's chosen 3 stats — already a SocialProfile.showcase field) → verified records strip (best lift per movement with tier badge — the profile's centrepiece) → training graph (consistency heat-map, 12 weeks) → posts (chronological cards) → connections. A coach's profile adds the storefront block (programs, rating, price note, "Start program") above posts.

Notification hierarchy

Two strata, hard-separated. Inbox — things requiring a decision: co-sign requests ("Adam asks you to confirm his 180 kg squat"), follow requests, coach enrolment requests, challenge invites. Activity — things requiring at most a glance: kudos (coalesced: "Kasia and 6 others gave kudos"), comments, mentions, matched-lift and rival events. Inbox items are cards with actions; activity items are rows. Badging counts Inbox only — a badge must always mean a decision is waiting.

Messaging hierarchy — the deliberate omission

No general-purpose DMs at v1. Messaging exists only as contextual threads: comments under a card, the co-sign request/response flow, and the coach↔client channel (which already exists as invites, enrolments and coach notes). Rationale: a DM inbox is the single largest moderation, safety and abuse surface a social product can add; it competes with comments for the same conversations while stripping them of context; and fitness DMs are the canonical harassment vector for women in this category. We add athlete↔athlete DMs only when a concrete loop demands them (e.g. Spotter Protocol pairing), scoped to mutuals, and that decision gets its own spec.

Exists vs net-new

Shipped: nav + Today pills, feed/discover/leaderboard/coaches screens on both clients, follow graph with pending states, blocklist, notifications API. Net-new: the three-tab feed split (today it's one list), gym entity + gym graph, clubs (planned), exercise pages, Inbox/Activity separation, last-read position.

What we deliberately do not build

Stories (authored ephemera — replaced by the Now-training strip, which is ephemeral state, not ephemeral content). Trending topics (a strength feed has no news cycle worth chasing; editorial cards in D4 cover the real thing). A "Nearby" public firehose (privacy hazard with no matching value — nearby expresses itself as a ranking feature and gym graph instead, D6). Reposts at v1 (quote-posting a stranger's PR is X's outrage mechanic wearing a singlet; saving + sharing-out cover the honest use).

Deliverable 3 — Anatomy of the post

D3

One card model, six zones, A through F. Every card type in D4 is a configuration of these zones — no card invents private structure. Zones render top to bottom; any zone may be absent, but never reordered.

Zone B — Headline

The moment, in display type — the one line that survives a 200 ms glance: "Back Squat 180 kg — new PR" with its tier badge inline; "First pull-up"; "12-week transformation"; "Won the March Volume challenge". Ordinary sessions get the session title in title size, quieter. The tier badge is the only badge that may sit inside a headline: T2 · witnessed — rendered as a chip, tap → the attestation sheet (who co-signed, when, the snapshotted claim — RecordAttestation is append-only, so the sheet is a receipt, not a claim).

Zone C — The figures

All figures read through deviceTrueSession() — measured beats typed, paces derive from exact seconds, never display-rounded values (device-truth rule). Configurations:

  • Stat row — up to four mono tabular stats: duration, volume, sets, kcal / avg HR (HR only when a device recording backs it). A subtle watch glyph marks device-measured figures — the "device signature".
  • Top sets — collapsed default for workouts: the 2–3 heaviest or PR-relevant sets ("Squat 5 × 180 kg", "Bench 3 × 122.5 kg"), not the full ledger. Expand → full block-by-block table with per-set loads, reps, RPE (RPE only if the author opted to share it — feel/fatigue stay owner-only by schema).
  • PR figure — the shipped prPostFigure(): big mono load + e1RM delta + spark of that lift's last 8 sessions.
  • Muscle map — compact anterior/posterior silhouette heat for physique-oriented cards.

Zone D — Evidence

  • Media — 1–4 photos (grid) or one video (muted autoplay in-viewport, tap for sound). Lift videos get an optional bar-path overlay later (tier 3 capture).
  • Charts — progression spark (this lift, 12 weeks), bodyweight trend, HR trace for conditioning. Mono numerals, endpoint emphasised, no gridlines louder than data.
  • Map — runs/rides only; start/end trimmed by privacy zones; hidden entirely unless the author shares routes.

Zone E — Words

Author's caption (280 chars soft target, expandable), then structured context chips: program link ("W4D2 — Soviet 8-Week"), club, challenge, event. Session.note is never here — it is owner-only by schema and stays that way; the caption is written for the feed or not at all.

Zone F — Action bar

ActionBehaviour
KudosPrimary. One tap, optimistic, haptic; the count is the only public number on the card. Long-press → who gave it. (Existing Kudos model, unique per user+subject.)
CommentOpens inline thread (shipped): flat with single-level replies. Count shown.
Co-signContextual, PR cards only, only if you were there (same-gym-hour heuristic): "I watched this" — the witness path into tier 2 from the feed side.
Match thisTraining-native share: adds this workout/lift to your planned session. The feed's most important action long-term (principle 8).
SavePrivate bookmark → Saved collection (workouts become templates).
Share outOS share sheet with a rendered share card (tier badge included). Quote-posting: not at v1 (D2).

Collapsed vs expanded

Collapsed (in-feed): A + B + C(stat row / top sets) + D(first media) + E(2 lines) + F. Target ≤ 420 pt tall. Expanded (post page): everything — full set ledger, all media, full thread, attestation receipts, program context, and the logged-vs-measured comparison panel (the one surface that deliberately reads the raw session beside the device recording, mirroring the session summary's exception).

Why 34 types don't mean 34 layouts

Every card is a zone configuration (D3). Engineering ships six layout archetypes — stat-led, media-led, chart-led, text-led, dual-avatar, module — and each type is a preset over them (see D10, where this collapse is made explicit). Adding card type #35 is a content decision, not a component.

Deliverable 5 — Interaction design

D5

Scroll, refresh, load

  • Scrolling — native physics, no scroll-jacking, no snap. The header collapses behind Liquid Glass using the existing --scroll-collapse mechanic (blur and saturation ramp with progress). Media plays only in-viewport; offscreen cards recycle (FlashList on mobile — already on the backlog as mobile-list-virtualization).
  • Pull-to-refresh — the glass lens stretches: the refresh indicator is a small Liquid Glass disc that descends, refracts the content behind it (the existing lens material: 10% film, 6 px blur, 1.25 brightness), spins as a rim highlight, and springs back on release with --lg-spring (0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1). New cards insert above with a "12 new" pill rather than yanking scroll position.
  • Infinite loading — cursor-paginated, prefetch at 3 cards from the end, skeletons never spinners. "Caught up" marker per D2; beyond it, older ranked content loads on explicit continue.

States

  • Skeletons — card-shaped placeholders mirroring the six archetypes (identity circle, headline bar, stat row blocks) with the app's shimmer; skeleton count matches viewport, never a spinner in the scroll.
  • Empty states — per surface, always with one action: feed cold-start (D2 #13), no notifications ("Quiet. Go make some noise — start today's session"), search no-results (offer invite link), Clubs pre-launch (hidden, not empty).
  • Offline — the feed renders its last page from cache with an ash "Offline — showing Tuesday 18:40" ribbon. Kudos, saves, comments queue locally and reconcile with server order on reconnect; queued items show a subtle clock glyph. Composer drafts persist. Nothing blocks; nothing is lost.
  • Errors — inline, specific, recoverable: a failed page load appends a retry card ("Couldn't load more — Retry"), never a full-screen error over content that already rendered. Failed actions revert optimistic state with a toast naming the action ("Kudos didn't send — tap to retry").

Deliverable 6 — Ranking

D6

Philosophy

Rank the moment first, the relationship second, the clock third — and cap the machine's favourite trick.

Engagement-probability ranking, left alone, converges on outrage and thirst; that is X's lesson. Strava's lesson is the opposite failure: pure recency buries a friend's marathon under nine commutes. Our function makes moment weight — an editorial judgment encoded as data — the leading term, and treats predicted engagement as a capped multiplier, never the leader. The feed's KPI is sessions-started-after-viewing, not dwell time (principle 8): we tune ranking against training outcomes, which no engagement-maximising feed can claim.

The score

score = M × (1 + A) × (1 + F) × E × D(t) — moment, affinity, fit, engagement (capped), decay.

TermSignalsNotes
M — momentCard-type base class P0 > P1 > P2 > P3 (D4), verification tier bonus (a T2 PR outranks a T0 PR), rarity (first-ever > repeat), magnitude (e1RM delta percentile within the author's history)The editorial term. Set by product, not learned; learned terms may only modulate within a class, never across.
A — affinityRelationship strength: close friend > friend (mutual) > following > none (shipped Relation ladder); interaction history (kudos/comments both ways, recency-weighted); co-sign history (a witness bond is the strongest edge we have)Two-way signals only — being followed by someone famous adds nothing.
F — fitTraining similarity (shared lifts, same program, same goal), strength proximity (author's e1RM within ±10–15% of viewer's on the card's lift), same gym, same city, same club/challenge, coach relevance (your coach ×3; coaches you follow ×1.5), competition relevance (same upcoming meet/event)Fit is why a stranger's card can beat a friend's: a same-gym peer matching your bench is more useful than a friend's ninth commute.
E — engagementPredicted p(kudos ∪ comment ∪ save ∪ match) from card features + viewer historyClamped to [0.8, 1.5]. The cap is the whole point: E breaks ties, it never sets the agenda.
D(t) — decayHalf-life per family: live 15 min; workout 18 h; PR 72 h; recap 5 d; question/discussion 7 d (answers refresh it); editorial 14 dDifferent content ages differently; one global half-life is why other feeds feel either stale or frantic.

Guardrails

  • Author diversity — max 2 cards per author per session view; a same-author third coalesces into "Marek trained twice more today".
  • Consistency over celebrity — a small boost for authors on active streaks and for posts-per-follower low accounts: the quiet consistent lifter surfaces, the engagement farmer doesn't.
  • Negative signals are absolute — mute-type and "show less like this" apply immediately and permanently until reversed; no decay-back.
  • Every non-followed card explains itself — "Same program, W4" / "Trains at Atlas Gym" / "±5 kg of your bench", in the identity bar's meta line. If we can't state the reason in six words, we don't show the card.
  • Following stays chronological — the ranked feed earns trust only while the unranked exit exists.
  • Cold start — before the graph exists: strength-band + gym + program fit only, quality-floored (verified moments and answered questions), geography-diverse. No engagement term at all for new users; it has nothing honest to say yet.

Deliverable 7 — Engagement loops

D7

Loops that return people to training, ranked by honesty. Global constraints first — these are product law, not tuning parameters:

  • Notification budget: ≤ 3 pushes/day default (user-adjustable), Inbox-class always wins the budget; everything else coalesces into digests.
  • Every push deep-links to a state worth arriving at. If the destination isn't ready (e.g. recap not rendered), the push doesn't send.
  • No synthetic urgency, no guilt copy. "Challenge ends tonight" is a fact; "Don't lose your streak 😢" is manipulation. We send facts.
  • Rivalry is opt-in. Comparison pushes ("Kasia passed you") send only between users who both enabled Rivals.

Charts

Sparklines and progressions follow one spec: 1.5 px line in the card's accent, area fill at 12% opacity, endpoint dot emphasised with the current value in mono beside it, no axes in-card (full axes on expand), faint hairline grid only on expanded views. Never more than one series in a collapsed card. All values device-true.

Dark, light, accessibility

  • Dark (Aurora) is the design-lead theme; light (Kyoto Hour) is a full re-voicing, not an inversion — washi grounds, pine accent, Mincho headings — already shipped and token-guarded by palette.test.ts.
  • Contrast — chalk on card ≈ 14:1; ash on card ≈ 4.9:1 (meta text ≥ 4.5:1 held in both themes); lime is never body-text-on-ink for long runs — it's a chip/figure colour.
  • Type scaling — both clients read the shared scale; web's px → rem migration is on the backlog (web-font-rem) and the feed ships rem-first rather than adding to that debt.
  • Screen readers — cards expose a composed label ("Kasia Nowak, Back Squat 180 kilograms, new personal record, witnessed tier, 2 hours ago, 14 kudos"); actions are real buttons in DOM/accessibility order A → F; charts carry text summaries.
  • Motion & transparency — reduced-motion swaps movement for crossfades; reduced-transparency swaps glass for solids (both fallbacks already exist in the codebase).
  • House rules apply — no middot separators anywhere (spaced en dash in joined meta lines), no decorative dots before section labels (SectionHead idiom: bold display title left, mono meta right), semantic dots only (live, legend, calendar).

Deliverable 10 — Critical review

D10

The document above, read hostilely. Each finding states the weakness, then the redesign it forced. Items marked open ship with the risk and a measurement plan.

Weak points

1 — The feed is buried a swipe deep open

Feed-as-Today-pill (D1) trades reach for context. Strava's feed is the home screen; ours is one tap further, and habit loops are exponentially sensitive to taps. Mitigation shipped in this spec: the co-sign inbox and Now-training strip surface on the Today dashboard as a compact CONNECT strip (the existing feed-preview.tsx, upgraded), so the feed's most alive content meets the user on screen one. Measurement: if feed DAU/app DAU < 35% after two months, we revisit the pill order (Feed second, Performance third) before ever considering a nav tab.

2 — Verification could read as a caste system → redesigned

If T0 posts feel second-class, beginners — who are all T0 — churn. Redesign, now in D8: absence of a badge is the T0 mark (no "unverified" scarlet letter); tier bonuses in ranking apply only within PR-class cards, never to ordinary workouts; first-ever cards carry no loads at all. Verification pressure lands exactly where it belongs — on big claims — and nowhere else.

3 — Two audiences, one feed (coach spam risk) → redesigned

Coach content is our differentiator and our spam vector: unchecked, every storefront floods the funnel. Redesign, now in D4/D6: coach cards are their own family with slate accent (instantly recognisable, filterable via mute-type), capped by the editorial-style frequency rule for non-followed coaches, and the coach rail appears at most once per session for coachless users only. Coaches earn organic reach through client-win and answer cards — proof, not promotion.

4 — Nearby + gym graph is a stalking surface → redesigned

The original brief asked for "Nearby". Combined with live presence, that is a real-world safety hazard, disproportionately for women. Redesign, throughout: no public nearby surface at all (D2); location at gym granularity only; delayed-location default on posts; the Now-training strip is close-friends/followers-scoped and per-session suppressible; gym membership is opt-in and hidden from search until the athlete confirms. Privacy review sign-off is a launch gate, not a fast-follow.

5 — Redundancy: 34 card types was over-specified → redesigned

Half the D4 taxonomy differs only in copy. Redesign, now explicit in D4's closing note: six layout archetypes (stat-led, media-led, chart-led, text-led, dual-avatar, module) are the engineering surface; the 34 types are presets. This also kills a subtler redundancy — separate bench/squat/deadlift PR cards are one preset parameterised by lift.

6 — Unnecessary complexity trimmed

Cut during this review: reposts/quotes (outrage mechanics, D2), general DMs (moderation surface without a loop, D1), stories (authored ephemera lifters won't make, D2), trending (no news cycle, D2), a separate messaging tab, and velocity metrics in zone C at v1 (vbt-capture is blocked on hardware; the spec references it nowhere in the collapsed card so nothing dangles).

7 — Missing opportunities, acknowledged open

Not in v1, deliberately: exercise-page segment-style leaderboards (needs equipment-identity to make cross-gym loads comparable — QR-anchored machines, normalized load); program-efficacy cards as a growth wedge (index is shipped; the share card is a fast-follow); Spotter Protocol cards (D4 #11 speculates ahead of the planned capability — it ships behind it); history import (Hevy/Strong CSV — the single cheapest way to seed profiles with content, already on the backlog).

8 — Scalability & performance open

Risks, in order: (a) fan-out — ranked-feed candidate generation must be precomputed per follow-graph shard; the current buildSocialFeed() is a query-time build that will not survive 10⁵ users — the ranking service is a real backend project, phased: chronological + moment boosts first, learned E term later. (b) media — video autoplay on a FlatList without FlashList virtualization (backlog: mobile-list-virtualization) will jank; that item becomes a feed-launch prerequisite. (c) web bundle — the feed joins the app-shell switch, so web-code-splitting graduates from backlog to prerequisite too. (d) notification fan-out — matched-lift detection is a pairwise scan; run it as a daily batch, not per-post.

9 — Privacy, restated as commitments

Owner-only fields (note, mood, feel, fatigue, raw device stream) never serialise to feed DTOs — enforced at the API layer and covered by tests, not by client discipline. Visibility is evaluated server-side per viewer (canViewResults() exists). Blocks are mutual invisibility everywhere including search, suggestions, matched-lift pairing and witness requests (the shipped attest API already checks the blocklist). Minors: no strength-band matching, no location surfaces. Transformation media is watermarked and excluded from OS share previews. Data export includes social objects; account deletion cascades them (schema already cascades).

What survives the review unchanged

The thesis. Verification-as-content is the one idea here no competitor can copy without our ladder; the workout-is-the-post kills the cold-start problem Hevy never solved; and ranking moments over engagement is the difference between a feed athletes respect and a feed they doom-scroll. Everything else in this document is negotiable; those three are the product.
