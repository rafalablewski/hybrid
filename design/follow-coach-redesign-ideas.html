Hybrid design study

Follow a coach — five repairs

The current rail suffers four sins: ragged chips (one card wraps its tags to two lines, its neighbour doesn't, so no two cards share a bottom edge), too narrow (220 px strangles the headline and review count), no sell (an empty initials-circle and two grey chips give nobody a reason to follow), and chaos (five type styles competing in 200 px of height). Each concept below removes something before it adds anything.

fix chip raggedness fix width add proof + invitation one geometry for every card
1The Ledger

Stop making cards

The honest fix: a rail of near-identical cards is a list wearing a costume. Full-width rows use every pixel of the screen, the chips collapse into one disciplined mono line, and the rating becomes a right-aligned column you can scan like a table. Nothing can wrap, so nothing can be ragged.

2The Byline

The discipline is the headline

Nobody follows a circle with "PN" in it — so the avatar is removed entirely. Each card leads with its single accent-coloured discipline, then the name set large, like a byline under a headline. Chips are gone; the footer is one pinned baseline: proof on the left, invitation on the right. Wider (280 px), and every card is the same height by construction.

3The Spine

Keep the card, impose the grid

The conservative repair. Every card gets four fixed rows — identity, proof, headline (one line, clamped), tags — so all bottoms align. The chip row can never wrap: exactly two chips, single line, and the first is tinted the coach's accent so the pair stops reading as grey noise. A 3 px accent spine gives each coach a colour without shouting.

4The Poster

Make the card sell

If the section must earn a follow, give it a storefront. Tall, centred, symmetric — a lit avatar, one discipline word, and a three-stat proof strip (rating, reviews, years) that replaces both the chips and the headline. The Follow button lives on the card, so the invitation is explicit instead of implied by a chevron. Narrow is fine when the layout is vertical.

5The Marquee

One hero, then a roster

Six equal cards means six equal shrugs. Feature one coach at full width — with an actual review quote doing the selling — and demote the rest to compact one-line rows. The hero rotates weekly, the roster rows share the Ledger's geometry, and the whole section finally has a hierarchy: someone to look at first.
