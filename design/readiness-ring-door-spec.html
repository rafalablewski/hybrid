Anatomy
  • A

    The kept arc

    67 points, in the readiness role colour from readinessRole() — lime at 80+, blue 60–79, amber 40–59, red below. It's the same ring the athlete already knows from Today, so nothing has to be re-learned.

  • B

    The deficit arcs

    One per cause, sized by points cost, in a fixed order — tissue, then conditioning, then the wearable, then the scale's own ceiling. Tissue is always the red, conditioning the blue, the wearable the amber — so a cause keeps its colour across every surface that names it.

  • C

    The verdict

    Six words, naming the top cause only. It is the one sentence that survives, because it's the one an athlete would say out loud.

  • D

    The consequence

    The existing nudge pill, unchanged, in the feeling's own accent. Kept because it's the only element that tells you what to do.

  • E

    The door

    Counts what's actually behind it — "3 reasons", never a fixed number. Opening it reveals the ledger the ring just drew, then the engine's sentences verbatim, then the provenance line.

Rules the arcs obey

Minimum arc. Anything under 3 points still renders at a 3-point width, and the shortfall is taken off the largest arc — so a −1 wearable nudge is visible without ever being mistaken for a big one.

Four arcs maximum. Beyond that the smallest merge into one neutral "other" arc, and the drawer itemises them. A ring with six slices is a pie chart, and a pie chart is not an instrument.

The kept arc is the only full-width one (10 against 7). It has to be, because the kept arc takes the readiness role colour and the tissue cost takes red — on a bad day both are red. Weight, not hue, is what says which part of the ring is still yours.

Order is fixed, not sorted by value. Kept, then tissue, then engine, then wearable — always. If the order changed with the numbers, the card would stop being learnable, which is the whole reason we're moving off prose.

Motion, and the amount of it

The kept arc sweeps once on entry (about 420ms, ease-out), the deficits draw after it in one 180ms beat. That is the entire budget. The sweep is doing work — it shows the deficit being taken off a whole — so it earns itself; anything more would be decoration.

Under prefers-reduced-motion the ring renders complete, no sweep. The drawer's open is a height transition on both clients, matching the sheet motion already used elsewhere.

What gets deleted

Three of the four sentences leave the default view. The fourth becomes the verdict. Roughly 110 vertical pixels come back on a phone, which is most of a card.

Nothing leaves the product: every sentence the engine writes is still reachable in one tap, still computed the same way, still refusing to invent a session on an empty log.

The risk worth naming

An attribution split can be wrong in a way prose never was. "Back fatigue is the main drag" is safe; "back fatigue cost you 22 points" is a claim the engine must be able to defend.

That's the price of the design, and it's the right price — a number that shows its work is worth more than a sentence that doesn't. But it means readinessDeficit() ships with its own tests before a pixel of the card does.

What building it changed — two findings the sum law forced out

Both were caught by the same rule, which is the argument for having it: a design that has to add up to 100 cannot quietly draw a cause the engine doesn't have. This page has been corrected to what shipped; the mock above is the real card.

Conditioning didn't move readiness — so we fixed the engine

The score was clamp(35..98, round(100 − avg(muscle fatigue) × 0.7 + wearable)). Conditioning doses fatigue.systems, never fatigue.muscles, so a week of hard running left the muscle average near zero and readiness near its ceiling. An athlete could run themselves into the ground and the number that prescribes their training wouldn't notice.

The ring found it by trying to draw the arc: the sum law refuses a cause the score doesn't have, and the arithmetic said its cost was zero. That's a modelling bug a card surfaced, not a layout question — so readiness now carries an ENDURANCE_SLOPE term at half the tissue slope (conditioning clears faster and limits the next session less), and conditioning takes a real arc in its own blue. A lifter with no conditioning in their log scores exactly what they did before.

Per-muscle arcs read worse than one tissue arc

Readiness averages all seven tissues, so splitting that term per muscle is arithmetically exact — and it looked wrong. On a normal week the fatigue spreads, so the biggest arc on the ring became an anonymous "other tissue", larger than the tissue actually named (measured: glutes 10, other 27).

The tissue term is now one cost, named by whichever tissue carries most of it — which is the same tissue the card's face calls the limiter, so the ring and the sentence above it can't tell two different stories.

A positive nudge takes no arc

When the wearable says you recovered it doesn't cost anything — it makes the whole deficit smaller. So a cost here means "this cause's share of today's deficit", which is the only reading under which the parts sum to the whole. The +3 still gets its sentence in the drawer.

The risk was real, and it was paid

"Back fatigue is the main drag" was a safe sentence; "tissue load cost you 30 points" is a claim. readinessDeficit() shipped with 52 tests, and the gate is one line: kept plus every cost equals exactly 100, across an empty log, a light week, a wrecking block, both clamps, and every wearable state.

Order of work

  • The door first, alone. Concept 07 needs no engine change — move the existing lines behind a disclosure, ship it on both clients, and the block drops from 38 words to 10 this week.
  • Then readinessDeficit() plus its tests, with nothing rendering it. The sum law is the gate.
  • Then the ring, replacing the disclosure's static ledger with the drawn one. The layout doesn't move; the ring simply starts carrying what the ledger was saying.
  • Then capabilities.ts, in the same change that ships each step — as the rule requires.

Sequenced this way, every step is independently shippable and the athlete never sees a half-built card. All three steps are now shipped on both clients — see the capabilities readiness-face-door and readiness-deficit-ring.
