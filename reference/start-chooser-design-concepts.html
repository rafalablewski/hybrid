“How do you want to start?” — five concepts

The first-run chooser a new athlete sees on Today, re-imagined in the app's Jony-Ive material vocabulary. Shared moves across all five: the floating “Start →” pill is gone (it competed with the choice — the choice IS the start), “Free” is said once, not three times (a repeated badge is noise, and per the one-accent discipline parallel items stay neutral), and every concept reuses Today's real anatomy — hairlines, crafted tiles, mono kickers, the chartreuse reserved for the single primary action.

Concept 01 — One Door

Recommend one path. Whisper the rest.

Ive's deepest habit: decide for the user, then make the decision feel inevitable. Three equal boxes force a choice; one hero card with the Go-Full glow anatomy makes “Follow a plan” the obvious door, while the other two paths become quiet hairline rows — present, honest, but not competing. The accent lives in exactly one place.

Concept 02 — Single Slab

One material, three cuts.

Instead of three floating boxes (card-in-card-in-card), the choices are carved from one slab divided by hairlines — the way Ive treated aluminium: a single piece, machined. Each row earns a crafted glyph tile in Today's deferred-row anatomy (ink tile, hairline edge), with the section-coding hue confined to the small glyph — never flooding the row.

Concept 03 — Three Materials

The Go-Full cards, turned toward the beginner.

Today already has a signature premium anatomy — tall cards, glow blooming from a corner, title up top, CTA pinned to the bottom in mono uppercase. Reusing it here makes the athlete's first decision feel as considered as the paid one, and the Spectrum section-coding (Train chartreuse, Feel teal, Plan sand) gives each path its own faint material without any badges.

Concept 04 — Just the Question

No chrome. Typography is the interface.

The most Ive move available: remove everything that is not the content. The question is set in the Fraunces serif at display weight (the editorial voice the Private-tab hero introduced); the three answers are pure type on hairlines — no boxes, no tiles, no badges. The chartreuse appears only on approach: hover an answer and the arrow arrives.

Concept 05 — One Control

Three paths, one button.

Ive's hardware instinct: one perfect control beats three competing ones. A segmented picker (the Liquid-Glass segment vocabulary, already native on iOS) selects the path; a single focused panel explains just that one; and there is exactly one Start button on screen, whose label adapts. The athlete never faces three CTAs — they face one decision, then one action.
