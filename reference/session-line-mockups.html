What the before is actually doing

It is offering. Three tabs, a greeting, a plan card, a week rail, four ways to start training, and a five-tab bar — every one of them a valid choice, none of them a recommendation. The screen knows the athlete slept 5 hours and it shows them a plan that assumes they slept 8.

"Train your way" is the tell. Four cards asking the athlete to pick a methodology is a question the app is better placed to answer than they are — and it is the block that gets scrolled past every day after the first week.

What the after gives up

Discoverability, and that is a real cost. The plan, the builder and the freestyle logger are all still there — they are reached by asking, or from Record — but a new user no longer trips over them. That is a genuine risk and it is why this is the screen to ship first and measure, not the one to ship last.

The struck-through 100 is the whole design. It says the plan said one thing and I overruled it, which is the only behaviour that separates a coach from a calendar.

The before is not bad — it is just expensive

Three numeric fields per set, a Save, an Add set, and a rest timer that must be started by hand. On a 20-set session that is sixty to a hundred deliberate taps, every one of them requiring the phone in a hand and eyes on a screen.

And look at what the RPE column does: it sits empty. The single most valuable field on the screen is the one nobody fills in, because typing a number about how you feel while out of breath is the highest-friction thing the app asks for. effort.ts is starved by a form.

What actually removes the taps

Not the microphone. The prescription. The system already knows the exercise, the set index and the load it told you to use — so the only new information in the whole set is what differed, and most sets differ in nothing. Confirm-by-exception is how a coach with a clipboard has always worked.

The three spoken words in stage 4 are not there to save taps. They are there to capture the RPE the form never got.

The same trick, in a different discipline

People eat roughly twenty meals on repeat. So the prediction is as good here as it is for a prescribed set: fill the day in from the athlete's own pattern and ask them to correct the deviations. Breakfast was logged before they woke up.

And the question changed. The before asks what did you eat — an accounting question. The after asks are you fuelled for the training you just did, which is the only reason a hybrid athlete tracks food at all. One number, on the day it matters, with the fix attached.

The fixes come from pantry.ts and the athlete's own logged foods, so the advice is something they can actually eat tonight rather than a macro target to go solve.

The camera is wrong here too, and for the same reason

Photo calorie estimation is the nutrition equivalent of camera form coaching: a beautiful demo, and unreliable at the one thing that matters. Portion mass cannot be recovered from a photograph — and unlike a missed rep, a silently wrong 200 g corrupts the fuel model without anyone noticing.

Barcode scanning is the opposite and it already ships. Deterministic, exact, and the camera is doing arithmetic rather than guessing. Keep it, extend it, and let voice cover the food that has no barcode.

Endurance is already zero-logging — and that is the point

The watch records the run. device-truth.ts already rules that the measured figures outrank the typed ones everywhere. So there is no capture problem left to solve here, which makes endurance the cleanest test of the whole thesis: with logging removed entirely, what is the app for?

The before answers "a comparison". Four lanes, eight weeks, four averages, and a control that cycles the sort order. It is a well-built chart that hands the athlete a question.

The one place real-time voice belongs

You cannot listen to coaching under a loaded bar. You can listen for forty minutes while running — which makes endurance the only discipline where "chat out" works during the effort, not after it. Everything spoken here is a ceiling or a correction, never a statistic.

The last screen is the company

"That run cost you 9% off tomorrow's squat" is the sentence no competitor can say. Strava has no strength model. Hevy has no endurance model. Garmin treats lifting as a rounding error. WHOOP measures the recovery and refuses to prescribe.

The cross-modal reconciliation is the moat, and it is the one thing here that is already built — the fatigue, readiness, effort and HPI engines reconcile both sides today. It has simply never been said out loud to the athlete in one sentence.

If only one screen in this whole document gets built, make it this one. It needs no camera, no microphone and no new science — only a voice and permission to state a conclusion.

The before already has every number

That is what makes this the cheapest change in the document. recap.ts computes the tonnage, the distance, the PRs and the deltas today, and the card renders them honestly. What it never does is reach a conclusion. Six figures and two chips, and the athlete is left to work out whether the week was good.

"+8% on last" is the closest it gets, and a percentage is not a conclusion — it is another number to interpret.

Not generative video. Rendering.

Every frame is deterministic motion graphics over real engine output with a synthesised voice. It renders server-side for cents and it cannot hallucinate. Generating photoreal video of the athlete is the opposite on all three counts, and one clip showing a rep they never did ends the brand.

It is also the most shareable object the company can make: the first fitness media that is about you and true. Retention loop and acquisition loop in the same 40 seconds.

The before is honest, and that is unusual

It already says the quiet part: the watch corroborated the session, not the barbell. Almost nobody in this industry admits that. Tiers 0 to 2 ship today — claimed, sensed, witnessed — and the co-signing flow works.

What it lacks is the shape of the thing. A single chip cannot show an athlete that there is a ladder, that they are on it, or that there is a rung above worth climbing.

The only camera bet worth taking

Not form coaching — attestation. Tier 3 is a narrow bounded problem: one declared attempt, framing the athlete wants to get right, one question — how many plates, and did the rep complete.

The failure mode is benign. "Couldn't verify, try again" costs nothing. "Your knee is caving" costs trust and invites liability.

And it is the only screen here with network effects. A credential is worth what its recognition is worth, which makes it a graph. The film is the most magical screen in this document; this is the most valuable one. Build it fourth.

Three tests, all three must pass

Decision-changing — it alters what the athlete does in the next 24 hours. Non-obvious — they would not have worked it out themselves. Defensible — the evidence fits on one line.

The velocity message is the interesting failure. It is true, it is flattering, and it changes nothing today — so it goes in the Sunday film where it belongs, and the athlete gets it with context instead of at 7 a.m. with none.

Structural guards, not a settings screen

Hard cap: one proactive message a day. If the athlete ignores three of a type, that type stops permanently — no re-prompting, no "are you sure". It arrives when they wake, not when it was computed at 3 a.m.

And if the day is unremarkable, say nothing. A coach who is quiet when there is nothing to say is a coach you believe when they speak.

The navigation

Screen 08
The most speculative one
Before — five tabs, sixty routes
TodayLogNutritionMessagesYou
  • The athlete navigates to find what the system already knows.
  • Dashboards present evidence and ask for interpretation.
  • Per-set manual entry is the default path.
  • Sixty routes, and the good ones are three taps deep.
After — one surface, one voice, one archive
NowRecord◉ hold to speak
  • Now — the claim, the session line, the receipt.
  • Record — verified lifts, the films, the archive. The trophy cabinet.
  • Voice — everything else. Authoring screens are reached by asking.
  • Plan editing, exercise browsing and food logging keep full traditional UI, unapologetically. Authoring is a screen job.

The honest caveat. This is the most speculative screen here, and unlike the other seven it is not backed by an engine that already ships. Navigation is not only wayfinding — it is browsing, discovery and accountability, and a product that hides its own data reads as unaccountable the first time it tells you something you disagree with. The AI decides the default; the screen exists so the athlete can inspect and override it. Ship the Today card first and watch whether anyone misses the tabs before deleting them.
