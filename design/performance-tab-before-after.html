The one-sentence argument

Today the four blocks that answer how am I doing right now take 16% of the scroll and the two absorbed screens take 68%. After, the state block is the largest thing on the page — it grows by 130px while the page shrinks by 76% — because that is the block the tab is named after.

What changed

What changed, and why

  • The number is named for what it measures. "HPI" is 100 − fatigue: it rises on a deload and rises further on a layoff. Calling it Freshness costs nothing and stops the page telling a resting athlete they are peaking. The band words follow it.
  • Capability is the missing half. One row — an eight-week trend of e1RM and pace, both already computed elsewhere in the app — is what makes the word Performance true. Freshness says whether you can train today; capability says whether any of it is working. A performance screen needs both, and today it has only the first.
  • Recovery stops being a third column. It is a ±15 adjustment, so it rides the headline as "wearable +4" instead of impersonating a peer index.
  • The limiter is a sentence. The one actionable word on the card gets the display face and plain language, not the tail of a mono caption.
  • The trajectory moves in and gets marked up. One card, one computation feeding both the figure and the line — so the last point and the big number are the same number. Session marks under the baseline make a dip attributable, which is what a trend chart is for.
  • Readiness is untouched. The ring, the computed "why" lines and the check-in nudge are the best-built things on the page. They ship exactly as they are.

What changed, and where it went

  • The hero stays; the screen goes back to being a screen. Seven columns, one figure, one verdict line and a door. The block ramp, the prescriptions, the seven muscle rails and the provenance ladder are excellent work — they are a programming tool with a chart grammar of their own, and they belong behind that door, entered on purpose.
  • The verdict line now names names. "1 over, 1 under" becomes the two muscles and the two numbers. The shape above it already told you that something was out of range; the sentence should tell you what.
  • "Your week" is cut. Today's activity card already owns the week — with real calendar weeks, a date filter and expandable columns. The two PR rows move there, where they belong to the week they happened in. That also retires the last thing keeping the demoted Statistics screen reachable.
  • "Breakdown" is cut. Three of its four tabs duplicate the exit rows verbatim. Its one unique line — the top driver — moved up into the state card, beside the limiter it explains.
  • Trends becomes a destination, reached from the exit rows, with a capped default and a virtualised table.
  • The editors become a settings route where an athlete can see what their edit did to the numbers.

What changed

  • Trends joins the rail as a door with the week's two figures on it — which is exactly the information the whole Trends sheet led with, at a fiftieth of the height.
  • Every row carries a live value. Velocity shows the profile it would open with. The coach row shows the question it would ask now, generated from the same data the page is already holding — a door that demonstrates instead of advertising.
  • The endurance row is the only endurance statement on the page, since the Breakdown card that duplicated it is gone.
  • No group markers. Six blocks in a deliberate order do not need signposting. If they did, the cuts were not deep enough.

What this is not

It is not a smaller version of the same page. Four things were added: a computed verdict in the masthead, a capability trend in the state card, session marks on the trajectory, and a real value on every exit. The page gets shorter because eleven cards were saying what another card had already said, or saying something that belongs on a screen of its own — not because analysis was traded away for whitespace.

The two corrections that matter most are invisible in a mockup and should ship first regardless of the layout: one computation behind the headline figure and its own chart, and one definition of "this week" across the hub.
