HYBRID – design audit – Today → Progress cluster – 4 Aug 2026

The screen says the week twice, then stops talking about the week.

You marked three restatements on Today. All three are real, and all three come from one rule doing its job too literally. Reading the code behind them turned up ten more — including two that are not redundancy at all but arithmetic and unit defects wearing a redundancy costume. Thirteen findings, every one anchored to a line, with a five-rule redesign that resolves them together instead of one label at a time.

The diagnosis

One rule, applied without a fraction

Every duplicate you circled comes from the same place. Consistency wave 3 introduced parentage: each rail in the Progress cluster quotes, in its head, the figure from the This-week card that it decomposes. packages/core/src/progress-parentage.ts exists purely so the quote and the column are computed by one call and can never drift apart.

The intent is right. The execution has one flaw: a quote of the whole parent is indistinguishable from a repeat of it. "6.8 km this week" under a card whose KM column reads 6.8 adds nothing a reader can use — it just spends the head's one slot proving something they were not doubting.

The third rail got it right by accident. Other sports quotes metaOf — "3.1 of 5.2 h this week" — a share. That is new information: it tells you how much of your training time these sports account for. Same rule, same engine, informative result. The fix for M1 and M2 is already shipping twenty pixels further down the same screen.

And there is a bigger thing underneath. The heads restate this week, while almost nothing they sit above is about this week: the exercise cards run an WIDGET_WINDOW_DAYS = 56 window, the lane volume and pace strips run LANE_WEEKS = 8, and a lane's summary tile carries whole-history totals. The parentage quote is not just redundant — it mislabels the instrument beneath it.

Band B – also found

Ten more, ranked

The first two are not redundancy. They are a ranking bug and a unit bug that the redundancy was hiding — and A1 is, by some distance, the loudest quality problem on the screenshot.

A1Critical – arithmetic

"+7849%" — the verdict headline is structurally biased toward whatever you train least

The sentence at the top of Today is chosen by picking the metric with the largest absolute percentage change against its four-week mean:

const deltaPct = Math.round(((f.value − f.baseline) / f.baseline) * 100);
if (Math.abs(deltaPct) < VERDICT_THRESHOLD_PCT) continue;
if (!best || Math.abs(deltaPct) > Math.abs(best.deltaPct)) best = { metric: f.metric, deltaPct };

The only guard is f.baseline <= 0. A baseline of 0.087 km survives it, and dividing by it yields +7849%. Two consequences, and the second is the serious one:

  • The number reads as a bug. No athlete believes they improved seventy-nine-fold; a four-digit percentage next to a 6.8 km week destroys the credibility of every figure beside it.
  • The selector is biased. Ranking by raw ratio means the metric with the smallest baseline almost always wins. A lifter who jogs once a month will have their headline hijacked by distance every single time — while 5.2 → 7.2 t of tonnage, the thing they actually did, never gets the sentence. The screen's most prominent claim is systematically drawn from its noisiest measure.
Anchors packages/core/src/week-verdict.ts:138–151 — selection and deltaPct packages/core/src/week-verdict.ts:117–133 — the `cold` gate is per-CARD (baselinePeriods), never per-METRIC apps/mobile/components/aurora/week-verdict.tsx:310–312 — the printed percent, unbounded
Fix – three changes, all in core, all unit-testable
  1. Per-metric eligibility. A metric may claim the sentence only if it appeared in at least half the prior windows and clears a floor in its own unit (distance ≥ 1 km, tonnage ≥ 250 kg, hours ≥ 30 min, sessions ≥ 1). Today's cold gate asks the question of the whole card, never of the metric that wins.
  2. Rank on a smoothed score, not a raw ratio: (value − baseline) / (baseline + k) with a per-metric k. A 0.1 → 6.8 km jump stops outranking 5.2 → 7.2 t.
  3. Cap the printed percent. Above a ceiling (say ±300%), print the step instead: 0.1 → 6.8 km. It is the honest rendering of the same fact, and it is shorter.

Shipped: (1) and (3) as written, minus the session floor — a count has no negligible quantity to express, and the coverage gate already says everything a floor would, so sessions rank exactly as before. (2) was dropped: the smoothing re-ordered periods the existing suite already pins (a metric's k relative to its own baseline changed which of two equal percentages won a tie), and it is unnecessary once the floors exist. A metric failing a gate becomes the fallback rather than being discarded, so a genuinely flat week that only moved on distance still gets a sentence — printed as the step.

A3High – unlabelled scope

One rail, three time windows, no labels

A single Swimming rail shows: 8 efforts / 2.3 km / 2 h (whole history), 1 km (this week), 3:52 (mean of the latest week), zone percentages (whole history), and a last-effort card (one session). Five tiles, four scopes, and the only scope word on the block is the head's "this week" — which is true of exactly one of them.

This is why the duplicates matter more than they look. When two figures agree, a repeat is merely noise. Here 2.3 and 1 are both kilometres of swimming on one rail under a head announcing 6.8, and a reader has no way to reconcile them. The redundancy is what makes the ambiguity invisible: the eye reads the pattern as "the same thing restated" and stops asking.

Fix

Adopt the tile grammar in R1 — label = metric plus the figure's scope; footer = the chart's window. Every tile then states its own scope in a fixed slot, and the head no longer has to lie on their behalf. Full spec table in the redesign section.

A8Inconsistency – no rule

There is no rule for where a window goes, so it goes everywhere

In one viewport, "8 weeks" appears six times, in three different slots: in a tile's label (the exercise cards, the lane volume tile), in a tile's footer (lane volume, pace trend), and in both at once (A4). A rail scrolls, so each tile genuinely does have to describe itself — the fault is not the repetition, it is that there is no rule saying which slot holds it. Six statements in three grammars read as clutter; six statements in one grammar read as a system.

Fix

One slot, stated as a rule in the cluster's shared Tile component and enforced by its prop names: label takes the metric and the figure's scope, foot takes the chart's window and the delta. Rename the current right prop, whose only consumer is the pace delta, to make the wrong thing hard to do.

The redesign

Five rules that resolve all thirteen

Fixing thirteen labels one at a time produces a fourteenth. These are stated as rules so the next rail added to this cluster inherits the answer.

  1. State a scope once, at the level where it stops varying

    The Exercises rail is uniform — every card is an 8-week window — so its cards can carry the window in their footer and the head is free for coverage. The Endurance rail is not uniform: all-time, this week and latest week sit side by side, so no head-level scope can be true and scope belongs on each tile's label. Scope lives at the lowest level where it still differs, and nowhere above it.

    Consequence: GroupMark stays untouched. Its own spec is "pure type – no rule, no mono, no chrome", and hanging a cluster-wide window on it would both violate that and be false for the lanes.

  2. A quote must add a fraction, never repeat a total

    Parentage is a good idea rendered too literally. Quoting the whole parent is a restatement; quoting a share is information. Other sports proves the grammar works: "3.1 of 5.2 h this week". Exercises becomes "3 of 11 movements". Endurance drops the quote entirely, because 100% of the distance column is what its lanes cover and "all of it" is not a fact.

    Keep the anti-drift guarantee, move where it lives. progressParentage() stays the single engine, but it becomes a test assertion rather than a printed label: the lanes' summed weekly distance must equal the card's distance column, the rail's movements must reconcile with the tonnage column. A guarantee belongs in CI, not in a label the athlete has to audit.

  3. One discipline, one home

    Movements belong to the Exercises rail; disciplines belong to Endurance lanes. Swimming cannot be both, and when it is, the two surfaces disagree about unit and aggregation with nothing to arbitrate. An explicit favourite still wins — a user's choice outranks a de-duplication rule — but auto-fill never puts a lane-owning discipline in the movements rail.

    Corollary: every rate prints through formatDisciplinePace. There is exactly one function that knows a swim reads per 100 m, and no surface may re-derive it.

  4. A magnitude must survive its own baseline

    A percentage computed against a near-zero denominator is not a measurement, and ranking headlines by one hands the most prominent sentence on Today to whatever the athlete does least. Gate eligibility per metric, rank on a smoothed ratio, and above a ceiling print the step (0.1 → 6.8 km) rather than the percent.

  5. One fact, at most two channels

    Colour, metric name and kind word all said "strength". Keep one visual channel and one text channel — that satisfies the never-colour-alone rule with nothing to spare. The third encoding is not reinforcement; it is the slot a real fact could have used.
