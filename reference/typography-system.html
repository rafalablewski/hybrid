This page is set in Söhne. Not a substitute — the files from apps/sohne-font-family, converted to woff2 and embedded in the page. Every specimen, every figure and every word below is the face the system specifies, so what you are judging is the real thing. All four cuts and all eight weights are owned; what the product uses is a decision, not a limit.

One thing to fix before this ships. The files currently in the repo are the TestSohne evaluation cuts, and those particular binaries carry 68 glyphs — space, comma, hyphen, period, 0–9, A–Z, a–z, Ö and ö. Nothing else. Every figure format this system defines needs at least one glyph outside that set, so 5:12 /km, 87%, 24 × 8 and 01:42:18 would each have drawn their digits in Söhne and their punctuation in whatever the browser fell through to. Thirty glyphs were therefore drawn to complete this document, each derived from the measurements of the specific weight it belongs to: the hyphen gives the stroke and the math axis, the period gives the dot, and composites like ä and ; reuse the font's own outlines. It is a stopgap with one correct fix — drop the full retail cuts in over these, which brings the several hundred glyphs the evaluation files omit and retires the synthesis entirely. Until that swap, judge the letters and the figures, which are Klim's, and read the punctuation as a stand-in.
01

Thesis

Philosophy

An athlete does not read this interface. They check it — mid-set, breathing hard, at arm's length, in bad gym light. Typography that has to be read has already failed. The whole system is built on one distinction, and everything else follows from it.

Language and measurement are different materials

A sentence is written by a person and is meant to be read. A number is produced by a barbell, a watch, a scale or a clock, and is meant to be recognised. These are not two sizes of the same thing. They want different rhythm, different alignment, different failure modes.

So they get different cuts of the same family. Söhne sets everything a human wrote. Söhne Mono sets everything a machine measured. That single rule decides the face at every call site in the product, and it means an athlete learns — without being told — that monospaced texture means this is a reading from the world. The interface stops needing labels to say what kind of thing something is.

Why one family and not two. Söhne Mono is Söhne — same skeleton, same weight axis, same warmth, redrawn on a fixed advance. Pairing an unrelated mono with a grotesque is a compromise nobody notices until the two disagree about what a 1 looks like. Here the figure and its label are cut by the same hand.

What it should feel like

Measured. Quiet. Structural. Confident. Unhurried. Exact. Physical. Durable.

Like a good instrument: the dial is legible before you focus on it, and it does not editorialise about the reading.

What it must never feel like

Loud. Motivational. Futuristic. Gamified. Wide-tracked. Gradient-filled. Congratulatory. A dashboard that is proud of being a dashboard. The product should never appear to be excited on the athlete's behalf — an athlete who put 120 kg on their back does not need the interface to be impressed.

02

The family

Cuts and weights

Söhne ships in four cuts and nine weights, and all of them are owned. We load a fraction of that, and the discipline is the point: every weight loaded is a weight somebody will eventually use for the wrong reason.

Cuts

CutVerdictJob
SöhneSHIPPEDEvery word. Headings, body, labels, buttons, prose, units.
Söhne MonoSHIPPEDEvery measured value, and the uppercase eyebrow that names one.
Söhne SchmalSHIPPEDTakeover titles only — hero (34) and above, on the four editorial surfaces in section 12. One weight, Dreiviertelfett 700. It is illegal anywhere else in the product, and the reason is measured below.
Söhne BreitOWNED, UNUSEDAvailable and deliberately not used. At 44 the two words "Personal record" measure 381pt against a 390pt phone's 366pt of usable width — it overflows a two-word title on the product's primary device. Its home is marketing, which this specification does not govern.

Why Schmal is a takeover cut and not a UI one

The obvious job for a condensed face in this product is the long name — the spec forbids shrinking type to fit, German compounds run some 40% longer than English, and "Bulgarian split squat" does not fit a card at subtitle. Schmal was proposed for exactly that and it failed the test.

Söhne Schmal is not a mild condensation. Its lowercase n is 311 units against the regular cut's 573 — a name set in it runs roughly 46% narrower. Two consequences, and both are disqualifying at reading size:

  • At the same size it reads smaller. That defeats the entire argument for using it. The point of a condensed cut here was to hold the size and give back width; at 16 it gives back width and visibly loses size, which is the outcome the no-shrink rule exists to prevent.
  • It cannot be mixed. A list where "Back squat" is Söhne and "Bulgarian split squat" is Schmal does not read as one list with one long item. It reads as two typefaces, and the athlete's eye stops on the seam.

At takeover scale it inverts. At 56 the same condensation stops reading as cramped and starts reading as deliberate — it is the proportion of a sports cover, and it fits "Your strongest week" in two lines where the regular cut needs three. So the rule is a floor, not a preference: Schmal is legal at 34 and above, and nowhere below.

03

Three directions

And the choice

Three complete systems, all in Söhne, all internally consistent. They are not variations on a theme — they disagree about what the product is.

A — Chosen

Precision

Back squat

120kg×5

Mono carries every measurement and every label that names one. Söhne carries only language. Tight display tracking, SI unit casing, uppercase mono eyebrows. Swiss, instrumented, cold on purpose.

Face alone tells you what kind of value you are looking at.

Columns align without effort; a rolling digit never jitters.

Scales to lab-grade analytics without a second system.

Reads clinical if mono is allowed to escape into prose.

B

Human Performance

Back Squat

120 KG × 5

No mono at all. Söhne's own tabular figures do the numbers, Fett 800 does display, size jumps are large and dramatic, Kursiv carries insight copy. Editorial, athletic, warm.

Feels like a brand rather than a readout. Strong marketing carry-over.

One cut, fewer files, simpler licence.

Every number now looks like a headline. Nothing recedes.

Uppercase units are typographically wrong and cost width in the one place width is scarce.

This is the look every fitness app already has.

C

Future Classic

Back squat

120 kg × 5

Three weights. No uppercase anywhere. No mono. Sentence case throughout, an eight-rung scale, generous leading, everything set in Söhne Buch and Kräftig. Extremely restrained.

Ages beautifully. Nothing here dates.

The most legible long-form reading of the three.

Sentence-case labels cannot hold a dense data screen — with no case or face contrast, a label and a value look identical.

Söhne's proportional figures do not column. A table of loads misaligns.

Restraint at this level reads as absence in a product about numbers.

Why A

Because this product is an instrument, and B and C both resolve the same question the wrong way.

B loses the distinction. When the load, the label and the heading are all Söhne at different weights, the athlete has to read the screen to parse it. Direction A lets them recognise it — mono texture means measurement, and that is legible from further away than any word.

C loses the column. Söhne's proportional figures are correct in a sentence and wrong in a table, and this product is mostly tables: sets, splits, macros, weeks. A system that cannot align a column of loads is not a candidate for a training app, however handsome the specimen sheet.

A is also the only one already true. HYBRID today has 1,220 monospaced call sites against 915 in the display face — the product has been a Direction A system for a year without anybody writing it down. A and B are not equally expensive: A is a face swap, B and C are rewrites of every screen that shows a number.

A's one real weakness — that it reads clinical — is not managed by borrowing warmth from B. It is managed by a hard boundary: mono never sets a sentence. Every word the product says to a person is Söhne, at reading weight, at reading leading. The cold is confined to the instruments, which is where cold belongs.

05

Weight

Four values

Weight is the primary hierarchy instrument in this system, above size and well above colour. The reason is practical: an athlete at arm's length in bad light loses colour discrimination before they lose weight discrimination, and a 13 px and a 14 px are indistinguishable at that distance while a 400 and a 600 are not.

WeightValueSöhneSöhne Mono
Buch400Body, long-form, secondary text, the × operator, disabled labelsQuiet figures: a table cell, a chart axis, a row's secondary number
Kräftig500UI default. Row titles, buttons, form labels, units, tab labelsStandard figures, the uppercase eyebrow, clocks and timers
Halbfett600All headings 16–26, the emphasised word in a rowPrimary figures — the value the screen exists to show
Dreiviertelfett700Hero 34 and above only. The wordmark.FORBIDDEN
A clock is 500, a personal record is 600. This is the whole weight philosophy in one line. Weight encodes whether a figure is an achievement or a readout. A running duration, a heart rate, a timestamp are the world reporting itself and take Kräftig. A load, a total, a PR, an index are the athlete's result and take Halbfett. Two weights, one semantic distinction, applied consistently across every screen — and it means a screen full of numbers still has a subject.

Never more than two weights in one component. A card with a 600 title, a 500 value and a 400 caption is already at the edge; adding a 700 anywhere inside it means the card is competing with the screen it sits on.

06

Leading

Ratios, never absolutes

Line height is a ratio, computed from the size at render. Absolute line heights are the single most common way a type system breaks accessibility: the OS scales the glyphs, the fixed line box stays where it was, and the text collides with itself before it clips. A ratio carries its leading up with it.

TokenRatioApplies toAt size
flush1.00A standalone figure in a tile or a hero. The figure is the box.46 → 46
tight1.15Display and hero titles, 26 and above. Multi-line mastheads.34 → 39
snug1.30Headings, list rows, table rows, labels, anything one to two lines.18 → 23
normal1.50Reading text. The default for body and caption.13 → 20
relaxed1.62Long-form prose, empty-state bodies, AI insight paragraphs.13 → 21
flush is the new rung, and it is the one that fixes tiles. A 46 px figure at 1.15 carries 7 px of empty line box it will never use — no ascender, no descender, no second line. Multiply that across a four-tile row and the grid gains a visible band of nothing that no amount of padding adjustment explains. Figures set solid; everything else breathes.

Density without cramping

The rule that keeps dense screens readable is that leading tightens before size shrinks. A set list that needs to fit more rows moves from normal to snug; it does not move from 14 to 13. Size is information, leading is only air, and giving up air costs the reader far less than giving up glyph.

07

Tracking

Relative, always

Tracking is specified in em, never in points or dp. This is not a preference. An absolute tracking value is a percentage that silently changes meaning with every size it touches: −0.5 is −1.7% at 30 px and −0.7% at 68 px, which is why a single title tracking token, applied honestly across the range, does nothing at all at the top of it. Every optical intent in this system is stated as the proportion it actually is.

08

Numerals

The core system

This is the part of the system that matters most, because it is where a training app is actually used. Everything below follows from one law.

The figure is mono. The unit is not. A measured value is set in Söhne Mono. The unit beside it is set in Söhne, at roughly two fifths the size, one weight down, in secondary ink, on the same baseline. The value is the subject; the unit is a caption attached to it. They must never be the same colour, the same face or the same weight, because at a glance the athlete needs to see a number, not a string.

What setting this in the real face taught us: the grouped comma. Söhne Mono gives every glyph the same advance, so at text.metric a comma occupies a full digit slot and 32,840 opens two visible gaps. That is correct behaviour and it is worth keeping, because it is the same property that makes 1,234 and 12,840 align in a column — but it is loud at 46 and quiet at 22. So the rule is a threshold, not a preference: at figure (22) and below, group with the locale's separator and let the mono rhythm stand; at figureLg (26) and above, a grouped figure of five digits or more drops the separator and takes a thin space instead. This is exactly the kind of thing that does not appear in a token table and does appear the moment you set the type.

The eight numeral laws

  1. Tabular by construction. Söhne Mono is monospaced, so every figure columns automatically. Where a figure must appear in Söhne proper — inside a sentence — it takes font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums only if it sits in a column; a number in prose stays proportional, because that is what proportional figures are for.
  2. One decimal place. Ever. 92.4, not 92.43. The second decimal is precision the athlete cannot act on and width the layout cannot afford. Analytical exports may carry more; the interface may not.
  3. Units follow SI case. Lowercase for symbols — kg km m s min h kcal. Uppercase for symbols from proper names — W N Hz. Uppercase for non-SI initialisms — BPM RPE TSS 1RM. KG is not a unit, it is shouting.
  4. Baseline, never superscript. A raised unit turns a measurement into a footnote and breaks the optical baseline of a row of tiles.
  5. Formatting is Intl's job. Grouping separators, decimal marks and unit position are locale properties. A hand-written comma is a bug in Poland and Germany, both of which are on the roadmap.
  6. Sign is in the figure, and is never colour's job alone. +8.4% carries its plus. Colour reinforces it; colour does not replace it.
  7. Decimal alignment comes free. Right-align a mono column and the decimal separators stack. No decimal-tab machinery is needed anywhere in this product.
  8. A figure never uses the accent. Chartreuse is the "go" colour and belongs to actions. A number is not a thing you can press.

Hierarchy

The order of instruments

Hierarchy is built in a fixed order, and each instrument is only reached for when the one above it has been exhausted. Reaching straight for colour is how a system loses its structure.

OrderInstrumentHow it is used
1FaceMono or Söhne. Decides what kind of thing this is before anything else is read.
2Weight400 / 500 / 600 / 700. Survives distance, bad light and motion better than any other axis.
3SizeThe 11 rungs. Adjacent rungs are not hierarchy — a level needs at least two rungs of separation to read.
4CaseUppercase mono marks a label as structure. Available only at 10 and 11.
5Colourchalk or ash. Two inks. Reinforces a level that size and weight already established.
6SpaceThe last instrument, and the strongest one for grouping. A rule or a divider is what you draw when you have run out of space to give.

There is no tertiary ink, and there will not be one. The palette has two text colours: chalk and ash. A third grey would have to sit between them, which puts it below AA on the card, and a level nobody can read is not a level. The only legitimate text opacity in the product is disabled at 0.5, and disabled is a state, not a level.

Tempo, RPE and the notations that are not measurements

A tempo — 3-1-1-0 — is a prescription, not a reading, but it is set in mono anyway. The test is not "did a machine produce it", it is "is this a value with a fixed format that a person parses positionally". Tempo, RPE, rep schemes and set counts all pass. A coach's note does not.

12

Editorial

Where the system relaxes

Four surfaces are allowed a more expressive setting, and the licence is narrow: they may use more size, and — at 34 and above only — Söhne Schmal. No new weights, no new tracking, no italic, and no condensed type below the hero rung. Expressiveness here is scale, space and one change of proportion, which is why these screens feel different without looking like a different product.

Schmal is confined to the title of these surfaces. The figure beside it stays Söhne Mono, because there is no condensed mono and because a measured value never changes cut — a number that narrows when it gets important is a number the athlete cannot compare with last week's.

Optical weight compensation

This is the part that a naive light theme gets wrong. Light type on a dark ground blooms — the bright glyph spreads optically into the dark around it, so the same weight reads roughly half a step heavier in dark than in light. A system that ships one weight per style will always look correct in one theme and slightly wrong in the other.

So the display weight is a token with two values, and it is the only token in the system that varies by theme:

weight.display  dark  600   Halbfett — bloom does the rest
                light 700   Dreiviertelfett — no bloom to borrow

Below 26 px the effect is under the threshold of perception and every other weight token is single-valued. At the hero rung and above, the size is large enough that bloom stops mattering and 700 holds in both themes.

Hierarchy never rests on colour, in either theme. Every level in section 09 is established by face, weight and size before an ink is chosen, which is what makes the light theme a recolouring rather than a redesign. Test it by rendering any screen entirely in chalk: the hierarchy must still hold. If a level disappears, that level was never designed — it was tinted.

Across devices

What scales, what does not

The instinct to scale a type ramp with the viewport is wrong for this product. A larger screen is not held further away — an iPad is held at roughly the same distance as a phone, and a desktop display is further but far larger. Optical size is nearly constant across the range. What actually changes is how much information fits, and that is a layout problem, not a type problem.

Everything at 18 and below is fixed at every breakpoint. A 13 px body is 13 px on a 6.1-inch phone and on a 32-inch monitor, because it is sized for the eye, not the panel. Scaling it up on desktop makes a paragraph read as a pull quote and pushes the line length past comfort.

Only display and metric rungs scale, because their job is proportion — a masthead has to relate to the width it sits on. And they scale by moving up the existing ladder, never by multiplication: hero on desktop is 46, which is text.metric, a rung that already exists. There are no intermediate sizes and no clamp() ramps anywhere in the product.

Density is the real responsive axis. An iPad shows more rows, not larger ones; a desktop shows more columns. Line length is the constraint that governs both: reading text is capped at 68 characters at every breakpoint, and a wider viewport spends its width on a second column rather than a longer line.

Accessibility

And where not to compromise
RequirementSpecification
Reading floorBody prose never below 13. Labels never below 11.
The 10 px exception10 exists only for uppercase mono eyebrows: short, tracked, high-contrast, and never carrying information unavailable elsewhere on the screen. An eyebrow is a heading, not content.
Dynamic TypeHonoured everywhere. Ceiling 1.4 by default; the 1.15 clamp is permitted only inside chrome with a declared hard height, and every use has to justify itself.
Contrastchalk on ink 16.9:1. ash on the card 5.23:1. Units in ash at 11 clear AA on every surface they appear on. Accent text clears AA on both grounds.
Uppercase limitNo uppercase string over 24 characters. Screen readers spell out long capitals, and sighted readers lose word shape.
LocalisationNo fixed-width label slot anywhere. German compounds run 40% longer than English; a label that cannot wrap will clip. Any label that can wrap has a leading floor of snug 1.30 so Polish diacritics have descender room.
Reduced transparencyText is never set over an image or a blur without an opaque backing. There is no legible-over-anything ink.

Three places where the answer is no

  • The figure never shrinks to fit. If a value overflows its tile, the tile grows or the value reformats — 12,840 becomes 12.8 t before it becomes smaller type. Autoshrinking a metric is how a system quietly abandons the one number the athlete came for.
  • Tabular figures are not optional. They cost a little width and they are the reason a column is a column. No screen may turn them off for space.
  • The 11 px floor holds under pressure. When a dense row will not fit, the row loses a field. It does not go to 10.

The 25 rules

Enforceable

Each of these is written so a test can check it. A rule that lives only in a document is a rule with a maturity date.

  1. Three cuts, one family. Söhne for language, Söhne Mono for measurement, Söhne Schmal for a takeover title. A face outside this family requires deleting one of these first.
  2. Four weights. 400, 500, 600, 700. The other five are owned and deliberately unloaded.
  3. Söhne Mono never exceeds 600. A monospaced 700 closes its counters at exactly the sizes where digits must stay distinct.
  4. One italic. Söhne Kursiv 400, in the ghost placeholder, nowhere else.
  5. Söhne Schmal is legal at 34 and above, and nowhere below. It sets a takeover title on an editorial surface. Never a figure, never a name in a list, never a UI label — at reading size it reads as a size drop, which is the thing the no-shrink rule exists to prevent.
  6. A measured value is always mono and always tabular. A number inside a sentence is not a measured value and stays in Söhne, proportional.
  7. A unit is never mono, never above 500, never larger than 0.42× its figure, never above 16 — the clamp binds at both ends, so the ratio is a target and not an identity — and never the same ink as its figure.
  8. Unit case follows SI. kg not KG; BPM not bpm.
  9. One decimal place. Anywhere in the interface, without exception.
  10. Numbers are formatted by Intl. A hand-written separator is a localisation bug that ships silently.
  11. Multiplication is U+00D7. Never x, never *.
  12. Never more than two weights in one component.
  13. Never more than three sizes in one component.
  14. Adjacent rungs are not hierarchy. A level needs two rungs of separation or it is not a level.
  15. Uppercase is only ever mono, only at 10 or 11, only tracked at or above +0.085em, and only up to 24 characters.
  16. Positive tracking never appears above 12. Letterspaced lowercase at reading size destroys word shape.
  17. Leading is a ratio. An absolute line height is how Dynamic Type breaks.
  18. Tracking is em. An absolute tracking value means something different at every size it touches.
  19. Body prose never goes below 13. Labels never below 11. 10 is uppercase mono only.
  20. One text.metric per screen. A second one means neither is the answer.
  21. Nothing exceeds 46 except the takeover figure, which is a panel rather than an element and is a single named constant.
  22. Hierarchy is face, then weight, then size. Colour reinforces a level; it never creates one.
  23. Opacity never makes a text level. The only legitimate text opacity is disabled.
  24. Chartreuse is the "go" colour. It never sets a heading, an emphasis, or a figure that is not an action.
  25. A figure and its unit share one baseline. Never superscript, never centred.
  26. A running clock never changes width. Zero-padded and fixed while live; unpadded once finished.

The review

What came out

The first version of this system had eight heading rungs, thirteen sizes, a parallel numeric scale, five weights and three text inks. Every one of those was defensible on its own. Together they were a system that would have been impossible to hold in a designer's head, which means it would have been abandoned within a year and the product would have drifted straight back to hand-typed numbers.

The questions, answered honestly

QuestionAnswer
Is it too complicated?It was. Eleven sizes and four weights is now near the floor for a product with this much data density. It is memorisable.
Are there too many sizes?Eleven is defensible, but labelSm at 11 is the weakest. See the deletion test below.
Are there too many weights?No. Four of the eight owned, and 700 is confined to two rungs.
Is a third cut a complication?It would be if it floated. Schmal has one weight, one job and a hard floor of 34, and it is the only thing in the system that may appear on four screens and nowhere else.
Does Söhne feel distinctive?Söhne alone, no. Set at these weights against Söhne Mono, on this ground, at these trackings — yes, and distinctively. The identity is the pairing, not the face, and Schmal on the covers is the one place it raises its voice.
Does it disappear into the product?Yes, and this is the strongest thing about it. Nothing here is a typographic gesture. Every choice is doing a legibility job.
Can an athlete understand it instantly?The glance test in section 11 is the acceptance criterion, and it is met.
Does it feel premium and timeless?It is a neo-grotesque and a matched mono at four weights. That configuration has looked correct for sixty years.
Would a professional sports organisation take it seriously?The SI unit casing, tabular figures and single-decimal discipline are the details a performance director notices. Yes.

Removed

Display XL / L / M / S and H1 – H4

Eight rungs for one job. Display and Headline were the same ladder wearing two names, and every system that carries both eventually has a designer choosing between Display S and H2 on feel. Collapsed to five heading rungs: hero, display, headline, title, subtitle. This was the largest single cut.

A separate Data scale

The brief asked for a dedicated numeric hierarchy and it was drawn: five figure sizes of its own. Then it turned out every one of them landed on a rung that already existed. Figures use the same ladder in a different cut — which is a better answer, because it means a figure and the heading beside it are optically related by construction rather than by somebody checking.

note (15) and heading (20)

Three reading sizes inside two pixels, and two section sizes one rung apart. Neither was chosen; both accumulated. 210 call sites move to the neighbouring rung and no screen changes visibly.

A tertiary text ink

Deleted before it shipped. Any grey between chalk and ash falls below AA on the card, so it would have been a level nobody could read. Tertiary is made by type — a smaller rung at Buch 400 — which is stronger, and it is why this system passes the chalk-only test in section 13.

Weights 800 and 900

The outgoing face's 900 has 294 call sites, so this is the most visible change in the migration. 700 carries a masthead completely. Everything above it is volume pretending to be hierarchy, and it is the single clearest tell of a consumer fitness app.

Mono 700

Drawn for hero figures, then tested at 46 and rejected: the counters close and 8, 9, 6 and 0 start to converge at arm's length. 600 is the ceiling and the figures are more legible for it.

Positive tracking above 12

Proposed for button labels. It is a decoration, and it costs the word shape a fast reader is actually matching against.

Responsive clamp() ramps

Replaced by rung promotion. Fluid type produces sizes nobody specified and cannot be checked against a token file, which is the same failure as a hand-typed number with better manners.

The one thing the review added

Every other line in this section removes something, which is the section's job. Söhne Schmal is the exception, and it earned the exception by failing first: it was proposed as the answer to the long name, tested at subtitle, and rejected — at 46% narrower it reads as a size drop and it cannot be mixed into a list. It came back only when the same test at 52 showed the condensation reading as deliberate rather than cramped. One cut, one weight, one job, one floor. That is the standard any future addition has to clear: a measured failure, then a measured success, then a boundary written tightly enough that it cannot spread.

Söhne Breit did not clear it. "Personal record" at 44 measures 381pt against a 390pt phone's 366pt of usable width, so it overflows a two-word title on the product's primary device. That is arithmetic, not taste, and it is recorded here so the cut is not re-proposed on the strength of a desktop mockup.

The next thing to delete, and the test that decides it

text.labelSm at 11 sits between caption (12) and overline (10) and is the one rung in the ladder without a job only it can do. It survives this round because it holds 400 call sites and cutting it blind is how a system gets a reputation for churn.

The test: take the twenty densest rows in the product and set every labelSm to caption. If nothing reflows and nothing loses a level, the rung is drift and it goes — ten sizes, four weights, two cuts. That is a system a designer can hold entirely in their head, which is the only kind that survives contact with a growing team.

The one thing this system still gets wrong. An athlete wearing gloves, in a squat rack, at 5 a.m., is not looking at a 13 px coaching sentence — and section 11's panel still spends a third of its height on one. The next revision should test a session mode where everything below bodyLg is simply absent, rather than present and quiet. That is a product decision this document cannot make, but the type system should stop pretending the question is settled.

Order of work

  1. Drop the retail cuts in. The licence is held; the binaries in the repo are the evaluation files, which carry 68 glyphs. Replace them with the full cuts, self-host woff2 on web, bundle the otf in the app — and delete reference/sohne-extend.py in the same change, because a synthesis kept past the moment it is needed is a second answer to a question the retail files already answer.
  2. Convert tracking from dp to em before the face changes. This is a no-op refactor against the current values — the existing dp trackings convert to almost exactly the em figures in section 07 at the sizes they are used — so it can ship and be verified independently.
  3. Add leading.flush and move standalone figures onto it. Visible improvement, zero risk, no font dependency.
  4. Retire note and heading. Mechanical, and it shrinks the surface the face swap has to cross.
  5. Introduce the named text.* styles and migrate call sites onto them. This is the step that makes the face swap a one-line change.
  6. Swap the face. With everything above done, it is one edit to two constants.
  7. Re-measure fixed-width slots. Söhne Mono's advance differs from JetBrains Mono's, and any layout that computed a width from a character count has to be recalculated.
Steps 2 to 5 are worth doing whether or not Söhne is ever licensed. They are the difference between a product with type tokens and a product with a type system, and every one of them is verifiable by a test rather than by an opinion.
