Exercise picker — five concepts

The "Pick an exercise" sheet (Builder, logger, everywhere) re-drawn to close the drift with the More → Exercises page. Shared moves: the 8px dot-before-name list is retired — rows adopt the Exercises-page anatomy (crafted initials tile, tinted by modality, shape hints on the right), category headers become Explore-standard section heads (bold title + mono count, no mono-uppercase kickers), and search always accepts an unknown name as a custom add. All five are live — search, tap, drill.

Concept 01 — One Library

The Exercises page, turned into a picker.

The straightest unification: the picker IS the library. Same initials tiles (tinted by modality — lime strength, teal endurance, violet conditioning; sports keep their glyph in the tile), same card slabs with hairline dividers, same Explore section heads with the count at the baseline. An athlete who has seen More → Exercises already knows this screen. Tap a row — it adds.

Concept 02 — Rooms, then Things

Choose the pattern first. Then the lift.

Ive's answer to a 200-item scroll: don't show 200 items. The first screen is eight calm pattern tiles — Squat, Hinge, Push, Pull, Core & Carry, Endurance, Conditioning — each a quiet room. Tap one and only that room's handful of movements appears, in the unified row anatomy. Search still cuts across every room from the top. Two taps, never a scroll.

Concept 03 — Your Bar First

What you actually train, one tap away.

The library is infinite but your training isn't. The sheet opens with a "Your recent" chip rail — the Smart decay-scored staples from the Exercises page, one tap each — then the full library below. For a returning athlete, 90% of picks end at the top row without a single keystroke; the catalog is the fallback, not the front door.

Concept 04 — The Index

No chrome. A typeset index with a finger rail.

The typographic cut: no tiles, no slabs — movement names as pure type on hairlines, grouped under serif display letters, with an iOS-style alphabet rail for one-thumb jumps. Modality survives as a small coloured word on the right (information, not ornament). The fastest surface for an athlete who knows exactly what they're looking for.

Concept 05 — The Composer

Pick five. Close once.

A routine is never one exercise, yet today's picker closes after every pick — open, tap, reopen, five times. Here the sheet stays open: each tap collects the movement into a tray (tap a collected chip to remove it), and one lime action adds them all in order. The unified rows gain a small ＋ ring that flips to lime when selected. Try picking a few.
