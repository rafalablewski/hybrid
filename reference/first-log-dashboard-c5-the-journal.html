The Journal — yesterday, today, tomorrow: one page

Grids are for planners; this user is a diarist. The Journal replaces the hero area with a short vertical timeline — yesterday's receipt above, today's entry in focus, tomorrow as an open slot below — the exact shape of how a plan-less athlete actually thinks about training. Every day is the same kind of slot: a logged day is a filled entry, an un-logged past day simply doesn't appear, an open future day is a dashed page waiting to be written. The three original choices become tomorrow's slot actions. And on day zero the journal has exactly one entry — today — holding the three cards. When a plan arrives, prescriptions are simply written into the future pages. Same book, different author.

The design argument

A calendar grid asks "which day is it?" — a journal asks "what happened, and what's next?" That is the only question this athlete has. By making past, present and future the same physical object — a page — enrolment in a plan stops being a mode-switch and becomes something gentler: the book starts writing itself one page ahead of you.
