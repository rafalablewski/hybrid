The Watch Face — the week is chrome, not content

The other concepts make the calendar appear at some milestone. This one refuses the milestone: the 7-day strip is part of the masthead — structural, like the date — from the second the account exists. It sits bare on the page under the greeting, above every card, in all three states. Day zero: ghost numbers, today's lime disc, nothing else — a quiet promise of shape. Logger: chalk ✓s land on it with each logged day. Plan: the same strip carries the prescription glyphs. The content below the strip is what varies — cards, receipts, or lifts — but the face of the product never changes. There is nothing to "unlock", so there is nothing to be inconsistent about.

The design argument

A watch shows all twelve hours even when you have no appointments. The dial isn't a reward for being busy — it's how the object tells time. Making the week permanent chrome removes the entire class of "when does the calendar appear?" decisions. It appeared when the account did. The first ✓ landing on an already-familiar strip is a small, private ceremony.
