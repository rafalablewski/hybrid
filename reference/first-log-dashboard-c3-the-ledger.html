The Ledger — the record is the hero, the plan is a whisper

This user is already training — five sessions by Friday, no plan, no problem. So the dashboard stops trying to convert and starts keeping score. After the first log, the hero slot becomes the Ledger: the week's count set huge (the "number is the card" treatment the Done-today card already uses), a 14-day rhythm strip beneath it, then today's sessions as rows — all inside the same card geometry as the shipped plan card. Structure is offered exactly once, as a sand-colored section-head action — "✦ Want structure? Browse →" — the same quiet right-side idiom as the Explore tab. State C is the shipped plan screen, verbatim; the Ledger simply yields the top slot and slides below it.

The design argument

Respect what the person actually does rather than what we wish they'd do. Someone logging five workouts a week is not "unonboarded" — they are a regular, and the product should look like their gym notebook, not like a brochure they keep declining. The offer of a plan belongs in the margin, where good manners live.
