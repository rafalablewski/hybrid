HYBRID — Today / the activity card — web + mobile

The colour points at the wrong thing, and the drawer is a card inside a card.

Two defects, one shared root. Colour in this card marks the metric the sentence is about — it is written once and never moves, so pressing a column changes almost nothing you can see. And because pressing is invisible, the drawer had to shout: it arrives as a second bordered, rounded card with a caret pointing back up at whichever figure opened it. Fix the colour and the caret and the inner border stop paying for themselves. One change funds the other.

Defect one — who owns the colour

three ways

One constraint shaped the original rule and still holds: a bad week must not read as a highlight. Colour here is semantic — chartreuse up, terracotta down, chalk flat — and it is deliberately not the brand accent. So "colour the active one" cannot simply mean "paint the active one green". Each option resolves that differently.

A. Selection gets its own channel

Conservative
Press any column

Tone stays on the named metric. Selection is expressed in value, not hue — the label rises from ash to chalk and a 2px rail draws in beneath the column.

Honest, and it can never miscolour. But it is not what you asked for: the active column is emphasised, not coloured, and at a glance green still wins the eye.

B. The verdict's tone travels

Rejected
Press any column

One tone — the card's own direction — moves to whatever you press. Simple, and it does exactly what "the active one is coloured" says on the tin.

It also lies. Press Hours and it turns chartreuse in a week where hours fell 20%. The card's one semantic channel would be reporting the selection's mood instead of the metric's.

C. Each column carries its own verdict

Recommended
Press any column

The active column is coloured in its own direction: Tonnage up in chartreuse, Hours down in terracotta, Sessions in chalk because +6% sits inside the flat threshold.

Active is always the coloured one, and the colour is always true. At rest nothing is selected, so the named metric holds the tone and the card looks exactly as it does now.

Why C is the one. It needs no new data and no new constant. Every figure already arrives carrying its own value and baseline, and "is this move worth a colour" is answered by the same VERDICT_THRESHOLD_PCT (15%) the sentence uses to decide whether the week has a story at all. Nothing new for the two clients to keep in sync.

The one thing C costs. Select another column and the named metric drops to chalk, so for that moment the sentence has no coloured figure tying back to it. The tie is carried by the words instead — the metric is bold inside the sentence and the step figure beside it keeps the tone. It reads as "I have moved my attention", which is exactly what happened.

Defect two — the card inside the card

three ways

The drawer holds a share bar, three activity rows and the sessions beneath them. None of that is a separate object — it is the long version of the figure you just pressed. It currently announces itself with a border, a 16px radius, a different surface and a caret: four devices to say one thing, "this belongs to the column above".

D1. Flush compartment

Recommended
Opens under Hours

The panel runs the full width to the card's true edges and takes its bottom corners, so it is the card's lower compartment rather than a tenant. One hairline, one step down in surface.

Border gone, inner radius gone, caret gone — three fewer edges, and the expansion still happens in place.

D2. No container at all

Leanest
Opens under Hours

A hairline and whitespace. The content simply continues on the card's own surface.

The cleanest answer available, and possibly too clean: with the session rows on the same surface as the figures, a long list stops reading as "inside the thing I pressed" once the columns scroll off the top of the screen.

D3. Push it out to a sheet

Not now
Press a figure — the card never grows

The breakdown moves out to the existing bottom sheet. Zero nesting by construction.

But it trades a glance for a modal. This card's whole promise is that a figure opens in place, under the number it came from; a sheet turns checking two columns into a four-tap round trip.

The recommendation, assembled

C + D1

Per-column tone plus the flush compartment. Press across all four figures: the colour follows your finger and stays true about each metric, and the panel below is visibly the bottom of the same card. The drawer's header now carries that column's own comparison — −20% vs a 6.4 h four-week average — which is the working-out for the colour you just triggered, printed where you triggered it.
