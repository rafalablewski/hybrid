Design — Aug 2026

The exit belongs at the end of the rail — seven ways to draw it

Every rail carried a lime See all › in its section head. Three endurance lanes stacked down Today put three identical accent links on one screen, each pointing somewhere different, and none of them where the thumb actually is: the reader is at the right edge of the cards, having just swiped to the end, and the only way to act on “there is more” was to travel back up and left to a link they had already scrolled past. It also spent the head’s right slot — which per the Explore SectionHead standard belongs to the section’s meta — and spent chartreuse, the reserved “go” colour, once per lane, until the accent stopped signalling anything at all.

The rule that replaces it: an exit belongs at the end of the thing, in the shape of the thing. A horizontal rail ends in a tail card. A vertical list ends in a door row. Below: what shipped, then seven other ways it could have been drawn — each measured against the same house rules (full-bleed rails, no decorative markers, accent reserved for “go”, scope stated where the figure is, never a middot).

Shipped — both clients

00The tail

The last thing in the scroller: a ringed arrow plate with a mono-uppercase label in ash beneath it — a destination, not a shout. It is discovered by the same gesture that exhausts the rail, and reaching it is how you know you’ve seen everything, which a header link could never tell you.

Revised Aug 2026 — it is no longer a card. It first shipped wearing the card form (ink2 fill, hairline, the rail’s own radius and shadow) on the theory that it should “belong to the row”. That was wrong twice over. Every card in a rail carries a thing — a coach, a recipe, a business, a metric — and the exit carries none, so a filled bordered box at the end read as one more item that turned out to be empty, and people counted it: six verified businesses where there are five. Worse, each rail sized its tail to its own cards, so the same door was drawn at five different widths, radii and shadows — a component built to make the exit consistent whose props made it inconsistent. Chromeless fixes both: one drawing everywhere, and the only per-rail prop left is the slot width, which exists for the snap grid (an odd-width final child leaves the last snap short and the tail half-cut), not for looks.

01The counted tail

A door that states what’s behind it

Same position, but the card earns its 126px by carrying a fact instead of a button. It prints the size of the destination — 14 swims — in the rail’s own figure treatment, with “See all” demoted to the footer beside the arrow. The tail stops being chrome and becomes the last data tile, which is the strongest argument for it existing at that width. The risk: in the endurance lanes the count is already the first tile’s figure, so this would restate lane.efforts at both ends of one rail — exactly the redundancy the summary tile was rewritten to kill. It’s the right idea for a rail whose length is not otherwise on screen (recipes, verified businesses, the coach marketplace).

02The peek tile

Show the next one, don’t announce it

Instead of a card that says there is more, the rail ends with the next real card, clipped and faded under a gradient veil, with the label set against the fade. It is the most honest possible signal — “more of exactly this” — and it needs no metaphor at all. It’s how a well-made newspaper page turns. The catch: it only works where the next item is real and cheap to render. On a lane whose destination is a different shape (the endurance hub is a table and full-size charts, not more tiles) the peek would be a lie about what’s behind the door.

03The overscroll pull-through

Nothing at rest — the gesture is the control

No tail card exists at rest. Drag past the last tile and the rubber band reveals a narrowing panel: the arrow rotates as you pull, the label flips from Pull for all swims to Release, and letting go navigates. It costs zero pixels, it is the most gesture-native answer on the list (Apple News, Mail, and every iOS list-to-detail transition work this way), and it makes the exit literally continuous with the swipe that reached it. The catch is discoverability and parity: a control with no resting state is invisible to a first-time user and to a screen reader, and a mouse has no rubber band — so web would need a separate affordance, which breaks the one-product rule the moment it’s the only exit. Viable as an accelerator layered on top of a tail card; not as a replacement.

04The end-cap rule

A margin mark, not a card

The rail terminates in a hairline vertical rule with the destination set rotated in mono beside it — the typographic idiom of a book’s running head or a chart’s axis label. It costs ~46px, adds no new card shape to the row, and keeps the rail visually a row of data rather than a row of data with a button on the end. It reads beautifully in the display grid. The catch: rotated type is a poor tap target and a worse reading experience at accessibility text sizes, and it doesn’t survive translation — Alle ansehen rotated in a 118px column runs out of room. Charming; not robust.

05The door row, under the rail

The block’s own existing anatomy

The endurance block already owns a door-row: the “All sports +2” expander at its foot, a full-width ink2 card with a 32px glyph plate, a display-face label and a mono count. Idea 05 gives every lane one of those, directly beneath its rail. The great virtue is that nothing new is invented — it is the cluster’s established “see more” object, and it is impossible to miss. The cost is vertical: three lanes × a 56px row is a whole extra screen of doors, and it breaks the one property that lets the endurance block exist on Today at all — adding to a lane widens its rail, it never lengthens the block. It also puts two nearly identical door rows within 60px of each other at the block’s foot, one meaning “more sports”, one meaning “more of this sport”.

06The exit rides the last tile

No extra card exists at all

The rail’s final data tile — Last effort, the one tile that is already about a single navigable thing — grows a footer strip under a hairline: All swims →. The rail then ends on content, with the door attached to the most navigational card rather than parked beyond it. Nothing is added to the row’s length, and the association (“this effort, and every other one”) is natural. The catch: the exit inherits the last tile’s conditional rendering — the Last-effort tile only exists when lane.last does, so a lane with no matched effort silently loses its way out. An exit that can vanish because an unrelated field is missing is not an exit.

07The title is the door

Delete the control entirely

There is no “see all” anywhere. The lane’s name is the link — the whole header row is tappable, chalk display face with a quiet ash chevron, exactly as a section title behaves on the App Store or in Music. It is the most honest object on the screen (the thing that names the sport takes you to the sport), it spends no accent, it adds nothing to the rail, and it removes a string from three languages. The catch is the one that started all this: the header is at the top-left, and the moment of intent is at the bottom-right of a rail you have just swiped to the end of. A chevron on a title is also a weak affordance — it is easily read as decoration rather than a control, which is precisely why the App Store pairs it with an oversized tap target and a lot of user habit we don’t have. Best as a second route in, alongside the tail card — which is what we may add later.

What every option was measured against

House rules

  • The exit sits where the intent is

    A reader decides they want more at the moment content runs out. Any control that makes them travel back to a point they’ve already passed is asking them to undo their own gesture.

  • Chartreuse means go, and only go

    Repeating the accent once per section is how an accent stops meaning anything. Ash and the card form carry a destination perfectly well; lime is for the thing you’re meant to do now.

  • A section head names, it does not act

    The Explore SectionHead standard: display-face title left, mono meta right, no marker dot. The right slot is for a fact or a state control — not a navigation link duplicated down the screen.

  • Widening is free, lengthening is not

    The whole reason the endurance block fits on Today is that a lane grows sideways. Any exit that adds a row per lane trades that away for something a 112px card already does.

  • The exit cannot be conditional on unrelated data

    A way out that disappears because some other field is empty is not a way out. It renders whenever there is somewhere to go, and never when there isn’t.

  • One implementation, both clients

    A gesture with no web equivalent, or a drawing copied into four files, fails the parity rule before it’s judged on looks. The shipped tail card is one component, imported by five rails, on two clients.
