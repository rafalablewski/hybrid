Nutrition — design study

What's left, while you're still looking for what to add.

Right now the calorie ring answers “how much is left?” once, at the top of the Nutrition hub — then you scroll past it into Diary, your meals, your products, the recipe rail, or the food picker, and the answer is gone. You're choosing food with no budget on screen. Five ways to keep the budget pinned to the top of every nutrition screen.

All five read from the same shared engine, so the bar can never disagree with the ring. Scroll inside the phones — the mockups are live, the bars actually stick.

The ring hands its number up as it leaves

Scroll back up and they retract in the order they arrived. Each capsule is the residue of a card you have already read — which is exactly the idiom Today already ships.

Design
Zero chrome at the top of the screen. Capture at bottom − 46, release 6px later so a finger resting on the threshold can't make it strobe. Tapping a capsule scrolls back to its source card.
Mechanics
A zero-height position: sticky wrapper with the bar drawn absolutely over it — the list scrolls under the blur instead of being pushed down by a bar that's empty most of the time.
Core
nutritionHudState(sources, scrollY, {prev}), modelled on today-rail.ts. Web measures with getBoundingClientRect, mobile with onLayout — identical thresholds.
Cost
Low. The pattern, the curves and the hysteresis rule already exist and are already mirrored on both clients.
Risk
In the picker and the library screens there is no ring to scroll past, so those views must pass bottom: 0 to force the bar captured from the first pixel. Miss that and the feature is absent exactly where it was asked for.
2Ring morph

The hero doesn't leave — it shrinks

One element, two states, no pop-in. Over the first 190px of scroll the 152px ring travels to a 30px mini-ring pinned left, the 38pt number lands at 17pt beside it, the card's border dissolves into the glass bar, and the three macro readouts rise in from the right. The summary travels rather than being replaced, so you never lose track of which number you were reading.

Design
A single scroll progress p = clamp(y / 190, 0, 1) drives every dimension. Nothing fades in; nothing is duplicated. The macro card below shows the same three numbers, so the rise-in reads as the card lending them upward.
Mechanics
Web: a sticky hero whose ring size, type size and positions are interpolated from p. Mobile: Animated.event on the ScrollView, scrollEventThrottle={16}.
Core
Only hudMacros(fuelToday(…)) for the values — the interpolation curve is one shared constant (HUD_MORPH_RANGE) so both clients collapse over the same distance.
Cost
The highest of the five. It is a real motion piece, not a component.
Risk
The native driver can't animate width or fontSize, so mobile needs scale tricks or the JS driver — the same driver the Fabric entrance workaround already lives on. Budget a low-end Android pass before committing.
3Fuel strip

Four columns, no choreography

The literal reading of the ask: a permanent 52px strip at the top of every nutrition screen. Four numbers in tabular mono, and under each a 3px track where the unfilled part is what's left — the budget is the negative space, so the graphic and the number say the same thing. LEFT is set once, rotated on the edge, so the word never repeats four times across a 390px screen.

Design
Over budget flips the column: a leading minus, the number in red, and the track overfilling past its end cap. One state change, no new layout.
Mechanics
The simplest possible. Web: position: sticky; top: 0 full-bleed via margin: 0 calc(-1 * var(--page-pad-x)). Mobile: stickyHeaderIndices={[0]} — no kit change at all. No scroll listener, no state machine.
Core
hudMacros(fuel) only — four slots, each with left, pct and over. fuelToday() already exists in engines/nutrition.ts.
Cost
One pass. Ships in a single change on both clients.
Risk
It spends 52px on every nutrition screen, including the top of the hub where the ring already answers the question louder. Fix with one boolean — hide the strip while the ring is on screen — not with a state machine.
4Projection HUD

What's left if I add this

The strip, plus a ghost. The row under your finger pushes its macros up into the bar: each number keeps its solid left value and grows a dimmed → after beneath it, while the track sprouts a hatched segment for the addition. Anything that would break the budget goes red before you commit. This is the ask taken to its conclusion: not just how much is left, but whether this food fits.

Design
Try Carbonara — the fat ghost goes red because its 44 g overruns the 38 g that's left. The bar names what it's projecting, so the ghost can never be mistaken for a logged total.
Mechanics
A NutritionHudContext at the nutrition root exposing setPreview(macros | null). Food rows call it from onHoverIn / onPressIn (web also on focus) and clear on out, blur, scroll and unmount. The serving stepper in the food sheet feeds the same channel, so the ghost tracks the dial.
Core
hudProject(fuel, delta) — pure and tested, so both clients ghost the same numbers and agree on what counts as a breach.
Cost
Idea 3 plus a context and one hook. Best shipped as a phase 2 on top of idea 3, not as a first cut.
Risk
Stale preview state is the whole risk: if it survives a scroll, a navigation or an unmount, the bar lies about the day. Clear on every one of those, and debounce press-in so a scroll-drag doesn't strobe ghosts down the list.
5Pull-down HUD

One line, or the whole panel

At rest, the least chrome that answers the question: a 40px line reading 1 156 kcal left with three 2px macro dashes right-aligned — enough to know you're fine, not enough to read the detail. Tap it and it expands in place into the mini ring, the three macros in grams remaining, and the training-fuel line. Tap again, or scroll, and it's a line once more. The list never navigates away.

Design
Progressive disclosure: the glance costs 40px, the detail costs a tap. Collapses on scroll so it can never sit open covering a third of the list.
Mechanics
Web: a real <button aria-expanded> over a grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr drawer, which animates without a hard-coded height. Mobile: an Animated height or LayoutAnimation. Instant swap under prefers-reduced-motion.
Core
hudMacros(fuel) for the line, and the panel reuses the existing hero-ring and macro-track components verbatim.
Cost
Low–moderate. The panel is composed of parts that already exist.
Risk
The tap target sits at the screen's top edge, where it competes with the back button and the iOS status-bar scroll-to-top. And a drag-to-open gesture would fight AuroraScreen's pull-to-refresh — so tap only on mobile, and consider dropping the drag on web too for parity.

What all five share

Whichever direction wins, the same groundwork lands first — and it lands in packages/core so the two clients cannot drift.

Core — one new module

  • packages/core/src/nutrition-hud.ts — NUTRITION_HUD_BAR_H, hudMacros(fuel) returning four slots (left, pct, over), plus nutritionHudState() for idea 1 and hudProject() for idea 4.
  • It composes fuelToday() from engines/nutrition.ts, which already backs Today's fuel widget and the hero ring. The bar therefore cannot disagree with the ring — the same targets, the same training-fuel adjustment, the same over-budget thresholds.
  • nutrition-hud.test.ts alongside it: empty day, mid-day, exactly on target, over on one macro, over on energy, zero target.
  • Export from packages/core/src/index.ts.

Clients — where it mounts

  • Both nutrition roots already compute today and targets once, above the view switch (nutrition.tsx) — so one <NutritionHud> rendered there covers the hub, the picker, the food page, your meals, your products, the verified-source pages and the recipe screens in a single insertion.
  • Web: position: sticky; top: 0; z-index: 30, full-bleed with margin: 0 calc(-1 * var(--page-pad-x, 16px)) and matching padding. Nutrition has no cover hero, so top: 0 is clear of the plans.tsx sticky rail at top: COVER_BAR.
  • Mobile: ideas 3 and 5 need nothing new — stickyHeaderIndices={[0]}. Ideas 1, 2 and 4 want an optional stickyTop slot on AuroraScreen so the bar draws over the ScrollView (the shape aurora/today-rail.tsx already uses), with onScroll merged into useNavScrollProps().
  • Copy: new keys under w.recovery.nutrition.hud.* in packages/core/src/i18n-web/recovery.ts — the parity test then forces both clients to carry them.

Recommended order

The ask is a utility, not a set piece — so ship the useful version first and spend the motion budget only once it's proven.

First

Idea 3 — Fuel strip. The whole ask, one pass, both clients, no new kit surface and no scroll listener. Gate it on one boolean so it stays out of the way while the hero ring is on screen.

Then

Idea 4 — Projection HUD. This is the part that actually changes decisions: “62 g protein left” is useful, “this burger leaves you 33 g over on fat” is why you opened the picker. It is idea 3 plus a context, so it slots straight on top.

Instead of 3

Idea 1 — Residue rail, if consistency with Today matters more than always-on availability. It costs about the same and reuses an idiom that already ships — but remember the sub-views have no ring to scroll past.

Later

Idea 2 — Ring morph as the polish pass once the strip has proven where the bar belongs. Idea 5's expansion is genuinely optional: Diary and Insights are already one tap away, so the drawer may be answering a question nobody is asking at the top of a food list.

House rules these respect. Full-bleed via negative gutter margins with matching internal padding, so the bar reaches the true screen edge like every screen-level rail. No middot as a separator anywhere in the copy — a spaced en dash where a string separator is unavoidable, real layout everywhere else. No decorative dot before a label; the one dot in idea 4 is semantic (it marks a live projection). And whichever lands, packages/core/src/capabilities.ts gets a nutrition-hud entry in the same change — shipped when both clients have it, or planned on the side that lags, so a parity gap is never lost in prose.
