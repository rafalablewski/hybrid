HYBRID — design exploration

History × Calendar — six merge concepts

Today History is a flat card list and Calendar is a separate load-shaded month grid — two screens telling one story. Each concept below merges them into one surface, rendered in the AURORA language (charcoal ink, chartreuse accent, Sohne + Söhne Mono). Chartreuse = lifting, teal = sport/cardio, cell shading = training load — same encoding everywhere. Every phone scrolls.

CONCEPT 01

Agenda Spine

One continuous stream: planned, today, done.

The purest merge. A pinned week strip (with load dots) replaces the month grid; tapping the month name expands it to a full grid overlay. Below runs a single agenda: upcoming plan days as dashed ghosts, a glowing TODAY group, then the past scrolling down. Rest days collapse to a hairline so training rhythm stays visible without noise.

History stops being an archive and becomes the bottom half of your schedule — the same screen answers "what did I do?" and "what's next?".

calendar fully absorbedplan-awarefamiliar (Apple Calendar agenda)
Best for

Making History the daily driver — one screen for past + future.

Watch out

Month-at-a-glance load view lives one tap away (expandable grid), not on the surface.

CONCEPT 02

Heatmap Ledger

Twelve weeks of load at a glance, list below.

The calendar's real job here was load shading — so distill it into a GitHub-style consistency heatmap: 12 weeks × 7 days, cells scaled by sRPE load. Under it, a hard stat row (sessions, tonnage, PRs, streak). Tapping a cell scrolls/filters the ledger below to that day; filter chips slice by discipline.

The list itself gains a date rail so scanning by day is effortless. This keeps History list-first but makes consistency — the thing hybrid athletes actually chase — the hero.

motivating (streaks)cheap to buildno future/plan view
Best for

Emotional payoff — the "don't break the chain" wall of green.

Watch out

Doesn't absorb scheduling; assigned plan days would still need the strip or ghosts from #1.

CONCEPT 03

Month Journal

The calendar IS the history — day sheet on top.

Calendar-first merge: the month grid becomes richer (per-day discipline ticks — chartreuse lifting, teal sport — plus a ★ on PR days and load shading), and the old History list moves into a persistent bottom sheet showing the selected day. Swipe the sheet up for the full breakdown, swipe the grid sideways for other months.

One screen replaces both: the grid answers "how consistent am I?", the sheet answers "what exactly happened that day?" — no navigation between them, just a thumb gesture.

zero redundancyspatial memoryrecent-first scan is slower
Best for

Users who think in weeks/months and want the two screens to literally become one.

Watch out

"Just show me my last session" now takes a tap; the sheet must default to the most recent training day.

CONCEPT 04

Week Chapters

The week is the unit of training — treat it that way.

Programs are written in weeks, so History groups into week cards: a 7-day load sparkline (the calendar, miniaturized into every card), weekly totals (tonnage, sessions, PRs, hours), then slim session rows with a day chip. Sticky month labels keep long scrolls oriented.

This turns the calendar from a place you visit into a texture that repeats through the list — every card carries its own micro-calendar, and week-over-week comparison happens by just scrolling.

matches how plans are writtenweek-vs-week comparisonindividual days less prominent
Best for

Athletes following structured blocks who review training week by week.

Watch out

A week card is denser than a session card — the slim rows must stay tappable and legible.

CONCEPT 05

Timeline Rail

A training diary with a spine.

A vertical timeline: date nodes on a left rail — dot size and fill encode load, teal marks sport days, and rest gaps render as literal empty space on the spine, so density of training is something you feel while scrolling. A month scrubber on top jumps through time (the calendar, reduced to navigation).

It reads like a logbook: the rail gives every session a place in time, and streaks/gaps become visceral instead of numeric.

most distinctive lookgaps are visible, not hiddenrail eats ~56px of width
Best for

A signature, editorial "training diary" feel no competitor has.

Watch out

Narrower cards — exercise breakdown lines need tighter truncation than today.

CONCEPT 06

Block Chapters

History organized the way training is programmed.

Instead of a date list, sessions group under the training block they belong to: "Soviet 8-Week — Week 2" gets a progress ring (3 of 5 done), its completed days check off, and its remaining plan days appear as dashed to-dos inside the same card — the calendar's "assigned" concept absorbed as unchecked rows. Sessions logged outside any plan collect under "Freestyle".

This makes History double as a program tracker: you see at a glance whether the week's plan is actually getting done, next to what you did on top of it.

plan adherence visibleto-do satisfactionweakest calendar replacement
Best for

Users on structured programs — adherence becomes the organizing principle.

Watch out

Purely date-based questions ("what did I do on the 9th?") are harder; needs a date view fallback.

Recommendation

Where I'd land

The strongest single answer is #1 Agenda Spine as the screen's skeleton — it's the only concept that fully absorbs the Calendar (week strip + expandable month grid) and the plan schedule (ghost cards for assigned days) into one stream, while keeping "most recent first", which is what History is opened for 90% of the time.

Then steal the two best organs from the others: the stat header from #2 (sessions, tonnage, PRs, streak — collapsible, or shown when the month grid is expanded) for the motivational punch, and the day sheet behaviour from #3 when a day is tapped in the expanded month grid. #4/#5/#6 are stronger as alternate lenses (a view toggle) than as the default.
