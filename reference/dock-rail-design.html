The dock rail: History and Plans are not the same control

Two screens dock a strip of chips beneath the collapsed hero. They look different because twelve properties were decided four separate times — and because one of the two has no rail slot to sit in. Here is the audit, six ways out, and a recommendation.

The root cause is structural, not cosmetic

Mobile's CoverScreen takes a rail prop; web's CoverHero does not. So on web, Plans hand-rolls its own position: sticky bar next to the hero — and that hand-roll is where ink 86% / blur 14 / z 29 comes from, against the scaffold's ink 88% / blur 18 / z 20. Every other difference below is downstream of four call sites each deciding their own chip.

Six of the twelve are outright defects: four break the web↔mobile parity rule, one breaks the kit's own 44px floor on three of four rails, and one is a rail that isn't in the rail slot. Five are drift — small, arbitrary, and cheap to pin.

Exactly one difference is real. History's chips select: one is always on, and the panel below changes. Plans' chips jump: nothing is ever "on", the shelves already are the categories, and the mobile source is explicit that these are deliberately not a tablist. Any option that erases that distinction is making the control lie.

Six ways out

Chips are live — click them

Each option is rendered on the same bench, at the same scale, so they are comparable by eye. All six assume the structural fix — web's CoverHero gains the rail slot mobile already has — because none of them survive without it.

UNIFY

One rail, one skin

Both rails become the same component with the same chip. Plans' categories gain a selected state driven by scroll position, so the chip you're standing in lights up as you scroll — the dead jump chips become a live position readout.

Fixes
  • Every row of the audit at once — there is only one chip left to diverge.
  • Plans gains a position indicator it currently lacks entirely.
Costs
  • A filled chip reads as a filter. Pressing "Endurance" and still seeing Strength above it is a broken promise.
  • Needs scroll-spy on both clients (IntersectionObserver / onScroll + layout y), and it fights smooth-scroll during a jump.
  • Reverses an explicit decision recorded in the mobile source.
CODIFY

Two roles, one vocabulary

Keep the visual difference, but make it a rule rather than an accident: a chip that selects is tint-filled; a chip that jumps is ghost. Then pin everything else — geometry, height, face, tracking, padding, hit target, rail chrome — so the fill is the only thing that differs, and it differs for a reason a reader can learn once.

  • Two states to keep honest in the guard test rather than one.
UNDERLINE

Tabs that look like tabs

History's four views are a fixed, exclusive, permanent set — the textbook case for an underline tab bar. Give it one: equal-width labels, chalk when active, a 2px accent rule beneath. Plans keeps the ghost chip rail. The two controls stop being confusable because they stop being the same kind of object.

SEGMENT

Reuse the Liquid segment

The app already owns a high-craft exclusive-selection primitive: LiquidSeg, built to the iOS 26 spec on a shared spring, shipped on both clients, currently used by the Today hub switcher and the week filter. History's four views are exactly its brief. Adopt it instead of maintaining a ninth hand-rolled selector; Plans keeps chips.

QUIET

Strip the box off both

Neither rail is the subject of its screen. Delete the pill entirely: mono uppercase labels in ash, the active one in chalk with a short accent rule under it, and nothing else. The "two kinds of pill" problem dissolves because there is no pill. The rail also gets ~14px shorter, which is 14px of History back.

  • The lightest rail of the six, and the least competition with the hero above and the headline figures below.

  • Doesn't remove the divergence so much as hide it, since the two rails still behave differently with nothing to signal it.
LEDGER

Put the figure in the chip

Every chip carries the number behind it — History's views show the session count they render, Plans' categories show goals per shelf. This is the only option that also absorbs the third rail on the Plans stack: PlanWeekRail, the waveform week picker, is already a label-plus-figure object. Make that the family, and it stops being the odd one out.

Recommendation

CODIFY, then LEDGER as a follow-on

Ship CODIFY as one shared DockRail primitive

It is the only option that closes every defect and every drift without asking a control to lie about what pressing it does. The rails should differ — one selects, one jumps — and the fix is to make that the single deliberate difference instead of one of twelve accidental ones.

Concretely, in one change across both clients:

The change
  • Web CoverHero gains the rail slot mobile's CoverScreen already has, and Plans stops hand-rolling its sticky bar. This alone fixes the chrome divergence and the z-index.
  • One DockRail + DockChip in the kit, twinned, taking role: "mode" | "anchor". It owns the full-bleed margins, gap, rail padding, radius, face and the 44px floor.
  • Selected = tint, not solid: lime @16% fill, lime text, lime border — mobile's AChip treatment wins, and web's solid-lime pill goes.
  • Weight stays constant across states so selecting a chip doesn't reflow the row.
  • Anchor chips keep no persistent selection, and keep role="button" rather than a tablist.
  • Guard it the way card-foot is guarded: one test over both client trees that fails on a hand-rolled chip in a rail, a sub-44px target, or a solid accent fill.
Touchpoints

Deliberately not in scope
  • PlanWeekRail's waveform stays as it is — it encodes weekly volume, which is data, not decoration. LEDGER is the path that would fold it in later.
  • No scroll-spy on Plans. If a position readout is wanted, it belongs to the shelf heads, not the chips.

Why not the others. UNIFY makes a jump chip claim a selection it doesn't have. UNDERLINE and SEGMENT both add a selection form to an app that already audited eight and collapsed them to two, and both clip under German and Polish. QUIET removes the affordance along with the divergence. LEDGER is the most interesting of the six and the right second move — but two of History's four counts are the same number, so it needs the view registry to carry its own figure before it can ship honestly.
