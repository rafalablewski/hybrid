The Ledger — the record is the hero, the plan is a whisper

This user is already training — five sessions by Friday, no plan, no problem. So the dashboard stops trying to convert and starts keeping score. After the first log, the hero slot becomes the Ledger: the week's count set huge (the "number is the card" treatment the Done-today card already uses), a 14-day rhythm strip beneath it, then today's sessions as rows. Structure is offered exactly once, as a sand-colored section-head action — "Want structure? Browse →" — the same quiet right-side idiom as the Explore tab's "See all →". When a plan is enrolled, the week rail takes the hero slot and the Ledger simply moves below it.

The design argument

Respect what the person actually does rather than what we wish they'd do. Someone logging five workouts a week is not "unonboarded" — they are a regular, and the product should look like their gym notebook, not like a brochure they keep declining. The offer of a plan belongs in the margin, where good manners live.
