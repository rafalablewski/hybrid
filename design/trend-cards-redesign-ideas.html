Trends — card system

Seven ways to make the trend cards say something.

Today all three cards are the same object: a mono capital kicker, a large empty rectangle, and the actual answer hidden in 10px grey at the bottom corner. The chart is the biggest thing on the card and the number is the smallest — which is exactly backwards. What the athlete came for is the figure.

Ive's test is not "is it minimal" — it is "does every element earn the space it takes". Ideas 1–5 treat the two chart cards, idea 6 treats the exercise table, idea 7 dissolves all three. A shipped design pairs one of 1–5 with idea 6; both clients get the same treatment in the same change.

What's wrong with it
  • The subject is the smallest text on the card. "56 sets" is set at 10px in the corner while 80px of height is spent drawing six bars that carry no information.
  • The chart lies. Every empty week is floored at 6px, so a week with zero sets and a week with four look identical — and the floor reads as a deliberate baseline rather than as nothing. A measuring instrument that cannot show zero is not measuring.
  • Two cards, one form. Sets and tonnage are the same eight weeks drawn the same way in two hues. Repeating the shape makes both weaker and hides the one comparison that matters: heavy weeks against busy weeks.
  • Colour has stopped meaning anything. Chartreuse is the kicker, the bars, every exercise name and the active period pill. When the accent marks everything, it marks nothing.
  • Machine chrome. Mono, all-caps, letterspaced titles on every card — plus — pressed into service as a spec separator — turn a headline into a part number.
  • The table doesn't rank or align. The ↗ column names nothing, weights don't share a decimal column, and a glyph (▲) is used where a real figure (+5 kg) would fit.
1The figure is the card

Number first, chart as footnote

Invert the hierarchy. The week's figure is set at display size with its unit and its change; the eight-week history shrinks to a 34px sparkline that supports the number instead of standing in for it. The kicker drops its capitals and becomes a plain label.

3True zero

An honest instrument

Keep the bars, fix their integrity. No minimum height — a week with nothing shows nothing, sitting on a real baseline rule. The current week is the only solid bar; the rest recede. A dashed mean line gives the eye something to measure against, which is what the empty upper half was always for.

4The line, not the bars

Training is a continuum

Bars imply eight discrete, comparable objects. A stroke with a soft fill says what is true — one continuous load that rose. The endpoint carries a dot and its own inline value, so the card's answer sits where the eye already lands, at the end of the line.

5Against your average

Answer the question, don't plot it

Nobody reads a bar chart to learn "am I training more than usual" — they read it and then do the arithmetic. So do the arithmetic. One track, one marker at the eight-week average, one sentence in plain English. The history stays a tap away, not a permanent tax on the screen.

6The table, quieted

Rank, align, and say the change

Names return to chalk so chartreuse can mean one thing again — the live selection. Weights share a tabular column and land on one right edge. The ▲ glyph becomes the figure it was standing in for: +5 kg. The period switch loses its filled pill for an underline that moves.

Fewer objects, not smaller ones

Three floating cards make the screen look like three unrelated features. Rules that hold across all seven

Titles are sentence case in the display face, not letterspaced mono capitals — mono stays for data, where tabular figures earn it. No card title carries a separator; the unit belongs to the number.

One meaning per colour

Chartreuse marks the current week and the live selection. Teal is the second measure. Nothing else is tinted — a name in the table is chalk, and change is signed text, not a coloured arrow.

Truth in the chart

Zero draws as zero on a real baseline. Peaks and averages come from the same engines the rest of the app reads, so a figure on this screen can never disagree with the one beside it.

Both clients, one change

Whichever pair you pick ships to web and mobile together, from the same shared numbers — Recharts on web, the same layout in React Native views on the phone.
