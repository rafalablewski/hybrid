The sport page,
reduced.

Every sport in the catalogue has a page, and the page has grown to eleven stacked sections of equal weight. This is a teardown and five first drafts, worked the way Jony Ive works a product: find the one thing it is for, then remove everything that is not helping it say that. Then one thing gets added — because it was the thing missing.

Rev 3 — what shipped
  • All of it is built and pushed except one deliberate hold. 3 416 core tests pass, the mobile suite and typecheck pass, and the iOS bundle exports clean. Nothing here has been on a device yet — the charts and the new screen want a TestFlight round.
  • The hero ships exactly as it was. Every draft that moved the figure into the cover was withdrawn before any code was written.
  • Records are in — best 5 km, 10 km and half; 100 m, 400 m and 1 500 m in the pool; 2 k on the erg. Section 04 is the rule the code actually implements, not a proposal.
  • The two charts stayed two. Volume is bars, pace is a line. The one piece held back is folding the intensity mix into the bars' fill — three densities of chartreuse in a 104dp bar is a daylight question, not a simulator one, so it waits for a device (capability sport-volume-intensity-fill).

The move

03 — Thesis

A sport page answers two questions, and they are in different tenses. Where am I? is the past — a record, read. What do I do? is the future — a prescription, acted on. Today they are interleaved across eleven sections of identical rank, which is why the page has two chartreuse buttons and no centre of gravity.

How a record is decided

04 — The new figure

This is the one thing in the whole redesign that the model cannot already compute, so it gets its rules written down before it gets a layout. The constraint that shapes all of it: a device recording is a workout summary, not a stream. DeviceWorkout carries duration, distance, heart rate and elevation — no laps, no splits, no route. There is no way to pull the fastest five kilometres out of an eight-kilometre run, and pretending otherwise would be inventing a figure.

Rule 1 — the ladder

The distances come from the catalogue

One new field on the sport record, beside metrics and pacePer, so sixty-five sports are configured in one place and no client decides what a benchmark is. A sport with no distances simply has no ladder.

Running 1 km, 5 km, 10 km, 21.1, 42.2
Swimming 100 m, 400 m, 1 500 m
Rowing 500 m, 2 000 m, 5 000 m
Cycling 10 km, 40 km, 100 km
Rule 2 — the match

At least the distance, and not much more

An effort counts for a rung when it covered at least that distance and no more than 5% over. Running 5.2 km and calling it your 5 km is safe — the extra two hundred metres can only make the time worse. Running 4.9 km and calling it a 5 km would flatter you, so it never counts.

d ≥ target && d ≤ target × 1.05

The figure is therefore a floor: never better than the truth, sometimes slightly worse. That is the right direction for a number you might quote to somebody.

Rule 3 — provenance

Measured, or typed, and it always says which

A rung filled by a watch carries the device lockup. A rung filled by the catalogue's typed marker carries TYPED. Both are shown, because a runner who knows their parkrun time should see it — but the page never lets the two look alike. Beat a typed claim with a measured effort and the mark changes on its own.

Rule 4 — the limit

What this is not

It is not Strava's best-efforts. Without lap or route data there is no segment extraction, so a 5 km inside a long run is invisible to it. Say so in the explainer sheet rather than let somebody discover it and stop trusting the number.

The upgrade path is free: if HealthKit lap events or workout routes are ever imported, they feed the same rung and the block's shape does not change — only its accuracy and its provenance mark.

AThe hero, as it ships

Same art, same rail, same 33px title, same count line, same Beginner accessory, same collapse. Nothing in this draft touches the hero system. Everything below it changes so that the hero's counts are the only place those counts appear.

BThe big rung, then the ladder

The sport's marker distance takes the 46px figure with its delta and its provenance; the rest are hairline rows. An unset rung is an invitation, not a dash — "Run one and it lands here" is a page that knows what you are working towards.

CTyping is a door, not a field

The one writable thing is a door row at the end of the ladder and it opens a sheet. The reading page never summons a keyboard, and the door earns its ring because it goes somewhere.

DVolume — bars, with the effort in the fill

Height is the week's distance, the fill is that week's easy/steady/hard mix in one hue at three densities. A whole section deleted and the bars say more. Fallback if the stack tests badly: plain bars and keep the split strip — the rest of the redesign does not depend on it.

EPace — still its own line, now in register

Separate section, separate grammar, same eight columns. The break is a real week with nothing paced in it — today that week is deleted from the series and the axis is captioned with someone else's date.

FBests keep only what a record is not

Fastest pace left with the ladder, which states it per distance. What remains are the three maxima a ladder cannot express — longest run, longest session, biggest week — on the page's own hairlines.

GAn effort that set a record says so

The one place chartreuse appears in the efforts list: the session that owns a rung. It ties the ladder back to the log without a second list.

HOne dock, one verb

The only chartreuse pill on the record page logs a session. The prescription's action lives with the prescription, one push away.

The pushed screen. Everything cut from the record, given a place where it is the subject rather than the ninth section — and where its start button is the dock, not a second pill competing with one.

AThe hero, as it ships

Same art, same rail, same 33px title, same count line, same Beginner accessory, same collapse. Nothing in this draft touches the hero system. Everything below it changes so that the hero's counts are the only place those counts appear.

BThe big rung, then the ladder

The sport's marker distance takes the 46px figure with its delta and its provenance; the rest are hairline rows. An unset rung is an invitation, not a dash — "Run one and it lands here" is a page that knows what you are working towards.

CTyping is a door, not a field

The one writable thing is a door row at the end of the ladder and it opens a sheet. The reading page never summons a keyboard, and the door earns its ring because it goes somewhere.

DVolume — bars, with the effort in the fill

Height is the week's distance, the fill is that week's easy/steady/hard mix in one hue at three densities. A whole section deleted and the bars say more. Fallback if the stack tests badly: plain bars and keep the split strip — the rest of the redesign does not depend on it.

EPace — still its own line, now in register

Separate section, separate grammar, same eight columns. The break is a real week with nothing paced in it — today that week is deleted from the series and the axis is captioned with someone else's date.

FBests keep only what a record is not

Fastest pace left with the ladder, which states it per distance. What remains are the three maxima a ladder cannot express — longest run, longest session, biggest week — on the page's own hairlines.

GAn effort that set a record says so

The one place chartreuse appears in the efforts list: the session that owns a rung. It ties the ladder back to the log without a second list.

HOne dock, one verb

The only chartreuse pill on the record page logs a session. The prescription's action lives with the prescription, one push away.

The pushed screen. Everything cut from the record, given a place where it is the subject rather than the ninth section — and where its start button is the dock, not a second pill competing with one.

What is left. A device round. Everything above is verified the way a sandbox can verify it — 3 416 core tests, the mobile suite, both typechecks, a clean iOS bundle export — and none of that has seen a phone. Three things to look at on the next TestFlight build: the ladder's rungs under Dynamic Type (the rung labels sit in a fixed 78dp column so the times align, which is the kind of decision large text punishes), the record sheet's keyboard behaviour, and whether the stacked intensity fill is legible enough to close the one deferred item.

Two calls made without asking, both reversible. A cyclist's headline is a distance record with the FTP as a secondary line, rather than the other way round — an FTP is watts, so it fills no rung, and which of the two deserves the 46px slot is a judgement rather than a fact. And on a rung where both exist, the better figure wins whatever its provenance: a typed 21:00 outranks a measured 22:41 until you actually run it. Say the word on either and both are small changes.
