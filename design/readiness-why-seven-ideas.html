HYBRID — design study — performance tab, readiness explanation

Seven ways to say it without a paragraph

The block reads 38 words of prose before the pill, and every word is a number wearing a sentence around it. readinessWhy() already returns lines rather than a wall, which was the right first move — but the fix for a wall of text is not a stack of text. It is to stop asking prose to do a chart's job.

Nothing here proposes hiding the truth. The engine's honesty is the product; the athlete gets the same four facts in each concept. What changes is the material they are made of — arc, bar, ledger, silhouette — and how much of the reading the eye can finish before the brain starts.

Simplicity isn't subtraction

Ive's line: simplicity is understanding the thing deeply enough that the form falls out of it. Deleting two sentences here just makes a shorter paragraph. Understanding that "81/100 back fatigue" is a magnitude gives you an arc.

The number should show its work

Honesty of material. Readiness 67 is 100 minus what's been spent. If the deficit is drawn, the explanation is not a caption on the number — it is the number.

One voice per surface

Right now the ring speaks, then a narrator explains the ring, then a pill re-states the consequence. Three voices, one fact. Pick the one that carries and let the others go quiet.

House rules that bind

No middot separators, no decorative dot before a label, mono uppercase for meta, and whatever ships here ships on both clients in the same change. Geometry lives in packages/core so web and mobile draw the identical shape.

What it costs
38 words of prose, 47 with the pill
4 figures, each embedded mid-sentence
~11 s to read, on a screen scanned in two
3 voices stating one state: ring, narrator, pill
0 of it is scannable — no figure sits in a fixed position, so the eye cannot learn where to look tomorrow

The deeper fault is positional. Because the numbers live inside sentences, they move every day: with no conditioning load the third line vanishes and the wearable line jumps up. An athlete opening this daily never builds a habit of where to look — which is the entire value of a daily metric.

01

The deficit ring

The ring stops being a progress bar and becomes an account of the missing 33. One arc per cause, sized by its cost, read clockwise. The sentences become a legend.

Reading the seven together

Three families, and they are not equally cheap. 01, 02 and 03 all need the same new thing: computeReadiness returning per-cause point costs, not just a score. That is a real engine change with a real test burden — the parts must sum to the whole or the card is dishonest — but it is the change that turns the paragraph into an object.

04 and 05 need no new maths, only new form. 06 and 07 need neither, and are pure editing.

If it were mine

01 with 07's door. The deficit ring makes the explanation and the metric the same object — which is the Ive move here, not a smaller font — and the door keeps every honest sentence for the athlete who wants the derivation. 07 alone is the correct ship-now step if the attribution work can't be scheduled: it takes the block from 38 words to 10 with no engine risk, and 01 lands on top of it later without re-cutting the layout.

Whichever wins, three things travel with it:

  • Geometry in packages/core, the way trajectoryPlot already works, so web and mobile draw one shape and the parity rule is satisfied in the same change.
  • The colours are semantic, not decorative — a tissue cost is always the red, the engine always the blue, the wearable always the amber, matching ROLE_COLOR so the ring agrees with every other surface that names the same cause.
  • The empty and no-wearable states are designed first. This block's best existing quality is that it refuses to invent a session on an empty log. A ring with nothing to subtract must say so as plainly as the sentence does today.
