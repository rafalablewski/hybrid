Three invariants

true on all eighteen screens, or the screen is wrong
INVARIANT 1

One empty grammar, four tenses.

Every day with nothing on it renders the same block: SF Symbol, the title "No training logged", one description line, then the actions. Not a bespoke text row on one screen and a centred illustration on another — one component, one string, with only the symbol, the description and the action changing by tense. It is the most repeated state in the app and it currently has three different drawings.

INVARIANT 2

Log a sport exists wherever a sport could.

Present on fifteen of eighteen screens — including the first run, every past day, every day that already holds training, and both failure states. Absent only from the future (nothing has happened yet) and from the sheet that is it. Today's card offers it in exactly one state.

INVARIANT 3

The accent is spent once, or not at all.

Chartreuse marks the one thing to do next. An open day spends it on Start a session; a day that already holds training spends it nowhere and both actions go neutral. Never two lime surfaces on one screen — the current card has three (pill, today-disc, sport label).

Shipped

branch claude/swiftui-ive-design-redesign — typecheck, 3 268 tests, iOS bundle green

Five of the seven moves are written. Build 1 is complete and Build 2 is done except for the day pager — the card has shed its chrome, the rail scrolls four weeks deep with a load bar where the tick was, a mis-logged session can finally be deleted from the card, and the two capabilities the study kept pointing at are live: log a sport on a day that has already passed, and the first native SwiftUI controls the app has mounted.

Shipped — invariant 1

One empty block, three tenses

core/empty-day.ts holds the tense logic (unit-tested — one title key in all three, and a past day can never offer the live logger); aurora/empty-day.tsx draws it once. The rail's two bespoke empty states are gone.

Shipped — invariants 2 & 3

A primary and a secondary

aurora/action-pair.tsx. The dashed ＋ tile is retired; Log a sport now sits on every empty day, today's included. The hierarchy is a fill, so a day that already holds training spends the accent nowhere.

Shipped — move 07

Log a sport on a past day

QuickSportLog takes a date; the rail hands up the viewed day at local noon so no timezone slides the record next door, and the save clamps at now. The card grid and the watch-import bar are untouched.

Shipped — native controls

DatePicker and Stepper

NativeDateField and NativeStepper in aurora/swiftui.tsx — real SwiftUI views, self-contained leaves, null off-iOS. A system date control is exactly where native styling is correct.

Shipped — move 01

One separation device

Border, shadow and elevation deleted; of three hairlines exactly one survives, the week/day seam. The floor's rule became 22dp of air — its seam already carries a mono-caps count that announces the change of subject.

Shipped — move 02

The rail scrolls, four weeks deep

LOGBOOK_SCROLL_WINDOW = 28, fixed 44dp cells, parked on today at first layout only. LogbookDay grew loadMin and load, device-true and normalised against the heaviest day in view — the tick's pixels now spent on how much. The LAST 7 DAYS caption is retired.

Shipped — move 04, in part

Swipe a row to delete it

The shared SwipeRow gesture over deleteSession, confirmed by the app's own sheet. Until now the only way to remove a mis-logged session was to open it — which is the wrong place to decide it shouldn't exist.

Still open — and why Move 06's day pager and rolling figures, and move 05. The pager is the one piece whose honest form (TabView wrapping the day's content) puts a native view in charge of CONTENT, and contentTransition(.numericText) has no faithful RN stand-in — a crossfade pretending to be a digit roll is worse than none. Move 05 changes native configuration, so it rides its own prebuild: one variable per build. The edge states (S3 loading, S8 in-progress, S13 offline, S15 undo) need data the screen doesn't carry yet — a request phase, a live-session handle, a queue depth, a restore endpoint — and inventing those shapes to fill a mockup would be the wrong order.
Deliberately not mounted ContentUnavailableView. Its grammar is what the empty block follows, but the native view styles its own type — SF Pro in the middle of a card set in Archivo — and its props are title / systemImage / description with no slot for the app's own buttons, so mounting it would split one block across two renderers and lose the house face for nothing. Native rendering of the two controls still needs a device round, as swiftui-kit already documents.

Not the glass. The view set.

verified against node_modules

Liquid Glass is a material — it changes how a surface looks, not what it knows. What is worth inheriting is the view set, and the installed @expo/ui@56 exposes nearly all of it. These are real SwiftUI views rendered by the system, arriving with their own separators, insets, selection behaviour, Dynamic Type response, VoiceOver semantics and — the part no hand-drawn React Native view ever gets — their own future. When Apple revises the list row in iOS 27, a List follows; a View with a borderRadius does not. The card currently uses none of them.

Seven moves

each trades a drawing for a view
01

The card becomes a Section

List + Section + listStyle(.insetGrouped)

Border, shadow and fill are three ways of saying "surface". Section says it once and hands over row insets, separators, header and footer slots, selection and swipe actions. Nested radii go concentric: each equals its parent's minus the padding.

  • −logbook-rail.tsx:91-102 border, shadow, elevation
  • +ASection in kit.tsx, RN plate as the off-iOS floor
  • +RADIUS.concentric(parent, pad)
02

The week scrolls, and ✓ becomes a Gauge

ScrollView + scrollTargetBehavior + Gauge

Seven flexed chips can only show seven days, which is why a caption has to explain the control's limits. A view-aligned rail makes history simply there. A tick says something happened; a capacity gauge says how much, in the same pixels, and speaks its value to VoiceOver.

  • −The fixed chip row, and w.home.logbook.window
  • +logbookDays(range) in core with a per-day load
  • +scrollPosition binding shared with the pager
03

One empty block, and a real hierarchy

ContentUnavailableView + two button styles

Every empty day renders the same component. Beneath it, borderedProminent and bordered on one baseline: Log a sport keeps its place, it just stops competing. Once the day holds training the accent retires and both go neutral — a fill change, not a layout change.

  • −done-floor.tsx:145-152 the dashed tile — the box, not the action
  • +AEmptyDay — symbol, title, description, actions
  • +AActionPair driven by one state prop
04

Rows become Labels, swipes come free

Label + SwipeActions + symbolEffect

An emoji can't take the row's colour, weight or optical size, and Apple redraws the set without asking. A Label takes systemImage and title as one unit. SwipeActions then makes a mis-logged session fixable — which the card has never been able to do.

  • +sessionSymbol(s): SFSymbol in core, emoji as fallback
  • +Edit (blue) / Delete (terracotta, behind a ConfirmationDialog)
  • −done-floor.tsx:116-118 the emoji tile
05

Give the screen edges back

Condensing large title + scroll edge effect

"Train your way" is currently smeared into a ghost under a hand-rolled blur. The system's soft edge computes its fade against what is actually passing beneath. A title that condenses into the bar returns roughly 120 pt of first screen.

  • +largeTitle slot on AuroraScreen
  • −Manual auroraScrollClearance() padding
06

The day is a page; figures roll

TabView(.page) + contentTransition(.numericText)

Today the detail remounts on key={sel.dateKey} and every figure hard-cuts. A paged TabView bound to the rail through one scrollPosition makes them one value. Digits roll in the direction of travel, held still by monospacedDigit.

  • −The key remount at logbook-rail.tsx:123
  • +ARollingFigure, honouring useReducedMotion()
  • +Haptic detent per page
07

Every past day becomes loggable

DatePicker, defaulted to the viewed day

A quick log always saves at "now", so the card cannot answer the most ordinary request an athlete has: I played on Saturday and forgot. The sheet opens dated to the day you were looking at — the one date the app can be certain about.

  • −The isToday gate at done-floor.tsx:145
  • +Native DatePicker + Stepper in quick-sport.tsx. The card grid and the watch-import bar stay exactly as they are
  • +quickSport takes a date → startedAt

Build order

four builds, each shippable on its own
Build 1

01 + 03 + 04 — the still frame (S1, S2, S4, S5, S10)

Pure JS, shippable over EAS Update. The card becomes a Section; the empty block lands as one component in all its tenses; the two actions resolve into a primary and a secondary; rows become Labels with swipe actions. Nothing animates yet and the section already reads native — this is the build that proves the direction on a device before any gesture work is paid for.

Build 2

02 + 06 + 07 — the rail, the pager, the past (S9, S11, S12, S16)

The week becomes a scrollable rail of gauges, the day becomes a TabView page, the figures roll, and the sport sheet takes a date. Land logbookDays(range) in core with tests before any UI touches it, and budget a device round for the paging/rail binding. This is where the new capabilities arrive, so it is where capabilities.ts gains its entries.

Build 3

The edges (S3, S8, S13, S14, S15)

Loading, in-progress, offline, failure, undo. Unglamorous, and the highest-value build in the set: four of these five states have no expression at all in the card today, which means the app currently answers a failed fetch and a rest day with the same blank space.

Build 4

05 — the screen around it

The condensing large title and the scroll edge effect change native configuration, so they ride their own prebuild rather than sharing a build with the card work. One variable per build, per the Expo-module rule that already governs this repo.

Two constraints that outrank all seven moves Every figure on this card must keep reading through deviceTrueSession() — a redesign is exactly when a surface quietly starts drawing typed values again, and S6 is the screen that proves it. And each move ships with its capabilities.ts entry in the same change; the mobile card is the golden standard on its own now, since the user-facing web client was retired in August.
