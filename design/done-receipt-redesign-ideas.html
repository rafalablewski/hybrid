Today — the logged day

Nine ways to read a finished day. One card, redesigned nine times.

The section is small and it is the most-read thing on the screen: what the day cost, in figures that can be vouched for. Each pass below applies one idea from Ive's working method — reduction, deference, honesty of materials, care for what nobody notices — rather than restyling all of them at once, so the trade-off in each is visible and arguable. All nine render the same real day: a swim and a tennis match, 4 August.

Never a middot

No ·, • or | joining values. Where a string separator is unavoidable, a spaced en dash. In components, layout does the separating.

No marker before a label

No dot, circle or square in front of a heading. Title left, mono meta right — the Explore section head. Semantic dots (status, device) stay.

The device outranks the typing

Every figure reads through the device projection. Derived rates use the watch's exact seconds, never the rounded display value.

An estimate says so

The ~ comes off only when a device counted every calorie. No bodyweight, no number — the stat is omitted, never invented.

Web and mobile, same change

Whatever ships here ships on both clients, off one shared model in done-receipt.ts. Nine layouts, one doneReceiptStats.

Where it stands today

A lime tick, a headline, the finishing clock, then a row of value-over-label pairs and a text link into History. It is honest and it is legible, and every figure on it is now a true total for the day.

What it isn't yet is resolved. Three facts of different kinds — one logged, one measured, one modelled — are set in identical type at identical size, so the card gives the athlete no way to feel the difference between a number the watch counted and a number we inferred. Every pass below is an argument about that.

01

The sentence

The labels exist because the figures were arranged in a grid; take the grid away and the units name themselves. The day becomes one line of language, read rather than scanned. Reduction in its strictest sense: three words of chrome removed, no information lost.

If it were mine: 03 with 04's honesty

The ledger is the only structure that doesn't degrade as the day gets more interesting — it takes a sixth figure the same way it takes a third, and it never wraps. The material rule from 04 rides on top of it for free: the modelled figure a weight lighter, its tilde in the gutter, one device mark for the day.

What it costs is height, and that is the argument to have. The row is compact and the column is clear, and on a card that already carries a week strip, a session list and a link into History, compact may simply be worth more than clear.

06 is the one I'd revisit separately. It answers a different question — not what the day cost but how it was spent — and it probably belongs to the session list below the seam rather than to the receipt.
