HYBRID – Motion – Waves 1–3 shipped, awaiting on-device sign-off

Screens should move, not blink.

This started as a proposal: every navigation was the same 240–260 ms fade-and-rise, so a tab switch, a drill-down and a modal looked identical. Waves 1–3 have since shipped on both clients — spring tokens in @hybrid/core, direction from hierarchy, a real paired exit, sheet parent-recede, shared-element continuity and masthead compression. The device on the right still runs the real app against a Now/Proposed switch, so it remains the fastest way to feel what changed. What's left, and why, is at the bottom.

Where we are

The foundations are good — reduced motion is respected everywhere and the nav lens is already native SwiftUI Liquid Glass. What's missing is a system: there is one entrance animation, reused for everything. Flip the device to Now and navigate around to feel it.

Wave 1 – no new dependencies

Foundation

Four changes that land most of the perceived improvement and need nothing new on either client. Press Play on each and watch the device.

Direction from hierarchy

Shipped

The nav map in packages/core/src/nav.ts already knows the app's shape. Derive the transition from it: the five bottom-nav destinations slide horizontally in nav order, a sub-screen zooms forward with the parent receding, a sheet rises with the parent scaling back. Today → Explore slides left; Explore → Today slides right — the app gains a geography.

slide 260msparallax 0.33×curve e-standard

Paired exit, not a hard cut

Shipped

Web currently animates only the arrival. The outgoing screen should leave on an accelerating curve — faster than the entrance, overlapping it by ~100 ms — so the two screens hand over instead of one blinking out. Flip the device to Now, navigate, then flip back to feel exactly what's missing.

exit 160ms / e-exitenter 260ms / e-standard

The list arrives as one thing

Cut on review

The first draft of this document proposed a staggered card entrance, 24 ms apart. That was wrong and it's been removed. Staggered list entrance is a Material idiom; on iOS a list arrives as a single unit and the navigation itself is the motion. Staggering also delays content for no informational gain, which collides directly with the rule two sections down that a transition may never gate input. The screen should be readable on the frame it lands.

See Audited against Apple's bar below

Sheets that push the world back

Shipped

Our sheet already slides correctly; the missing half is the presenting screen. It should scale to 0.92 and grow its corner radius to match the device, so the sheet sits on a real stack. The scrim then does far less work — it drops from 0.6 to 0.28 — and the nav pill retreats instead of floating over the sheet.

recede 0.92radius 0 → 30pxspring 380ms

The fourth item has no demo, because it's a file. Every number on this page should live in packages/core/src/motion.ts and be consumed by both clients, exactly as theme/tokens.ts is today — otherwise the two clients drift within a release and the parity rule is enforced by memory instead of by the compiler.

Wave 2 – the headline

Continuity

This is what makes it feel like iOS 26 rather than a well-animated website — and it's where the real engineering sits, because continuity means measuring an element in one screen and re-projecting it in the next.

The cover becomes the screen

Mechanism shipped

Tap a plan cover in the device. Instead of a new screen fading over it, the cover itself expands to fill the display — its corner radius unwinding, its oversized discipline glyph counter-parallaxing as it grows, the list behind receding — and the plan's own content staggers in beneath it. Back collapses it into the same cover. This is the single biggest perceived-quality win available.

zoom 440ms / e-zoomradius 28 → 0parent 1.06 / 0.35

The number travels

Shipped

A cheaper variant, and better suited to a training app. Rather than morphing whole cards, keep continuity on the datum: the 102 kg on the Bench Press widget is the same element as the big estimated-1RM on the detail screen. It scales and travels into place while everything else crossfades under it. It reads as data integrity, not decoration.

travel 440ms / e-zoomcrossfade 200ms

Interruptible back, with a live preview

Shipped on mobile

Open any detail screen and drag from the left edge of the device. The previous screen is real and already there, moving at one-third parallax behind your finger, with a shadow separating the planes. Release past 35 % and it commits; release short and it springs back. Nothing here is a canned animation — position is a function of the gesture.

Wave 3 – material

Polish that lives in the chrome

Individually small. Together they're the difference between "animated" and "physical".

Nav lens morph and merge

Half shipped

The travelling, stretching lens already ships and is the best motion in the app — it's live in the device, stretch and all. Two extensions: on Train the lens should flow into the centre FAB rather than fading out in place, and once you scroll the pill should merge into a compact capsule rather than only scaling down.

spring .32 / .74stretch 0.3 × distance

Glass reacts to content, not to itself

Cut on review

The first draft added a specular highlight that swept across the nav pill on every transition. Removed. Real Liquid Glass refracts and brightens according to what passes behind it — it doesn't emit a highlight on navigation. A flourish that fires every single time you move is decoration announcing itself, and it's the exact thing that separates a considered material from a themed one. What stays is the honest version, already in the pill: a live backdrop-filter so the content genuinely shows through and shifts as screens move underneath.

blur 18pxsaturate 1.2no scripted highlight

Continuous masthead compression

Shipped

Scroll inside Today. The big Today headline shrinks and a compact frosted header resolves in above it — continuously, as a function of scroll offset, not a stepped swap. The plumbing exists: lib/nav-scroll.tsx already publishes this exact signal for the nav pill. It just needs a second subscriber.

range 0 → 48pxtitle 34 → 14px

The world stays put

No new deps

The ambient gradient field (lg-field on web, AuroraField on mobile) already renders outside the screen wrapper, so it survives a screen change untouched. Give it a slow counter-drift during transitions and it becomes the fixed world the screens move through, rather than wallpaper. Nearly free, and it's the detail that makes a transition feel spatial — it's running behind every screen in the device.

counter-drift 0.12×settle 900ms

Haptics on commit, focus mode, celebration handoff

Not started

Two that can't be shown in a browser, and one correction. Haptics: far less than the first draft proposed. iOS fires nothing on ordinary navigation — haptics are reserved for a state change with consequence. So: the interruptible-back threshold crossing (a real detent) and set completion in the logger, and nothing else. A buzz on every drill-down would feel cheap within a day. Focus mode: entering the live logger already hides the nav pill, so make the entry a statement — the world desaturates and blurs out over 400 ms and the logger rises through it. Celebration handoff: the PR keyframes already exist (prTrophyPop, prSweep, prConfetti in globals.css:472) — wire them to the transition into Done so the badge flies from the set row into the summary.

The system

Motion tokens

The proposed contents of packages/core/src/motion.ts — and the biggest thing the Apple-bar audit changed. Anything positional is a spring, not a duration and a bezier. A bezier carries no velocity, so it cannot be interrupted or retargeted mid-flight, which makes "every transition tracks the finger" impossible to actually honour. Springs are also what the two clients can genuinely share: these are SwiftUI's own response / dampingFraction parameters, integrated into a CSS linear() easing on web so both platforms run the same curve rather than an eyeballed approximation of each other. Beziers survive only for opacity. These are the exact values the device is running.

spring.zoomresponse .52 / damping .92Shared-element zoom, number travel
spring.sheetresponse .46 / damping .86Sheets, modals, parent recede
spring.navresponse .32 / damping .74The shipped nav lens (global-nav.tsx:422). Unchanged.
ease.fadecubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1)Opacity only — nothing positional, nothing to interrupt
ease.exitcubic-bezier(.4,0,.9,.4)Dismissals — accelerating, things leave faster than they arrive

Review

Audited against Apple's bar

This document was re-reviewed against Apple's own motion practice — the Human Interface Guidelines on motion and accessibility, and how iOS 26 actually implements Liquid Glass and zoom navigation. Five things failed and have been changed. They're listed here rather than quietly fixed, because the reasoning is the useful part.

Duration + bezier
Failed. The first token table was four durations and four cubic-beziers, including one named ease.spring that was a bezier imitating a spring. A bezier has no velocity state, so it can't be interrupted or retargeted — while the same document claimed interruptibility as a headline principle. Self-contradictory. Now: real springs (SwiftUI response / dampingFraction) integrated to a CSS linear() curve. Telling detail — the one piece already shipping, the nav lens, was already a real spring. The new tokens had regressed from the code.
Rewritten
Reduce Motion
Failed, and it was an accessibility defect. "Resolve to the end state instantly" and "zeroed durations" delete the change signal — the user can no longer tell the screen changed. Apple substitutes a cross-dissolve. Now a 150 ms dissolve, applied throughout.
Rewritten
Specular sweep
Cut. Added in the second draft and described as beautiful. It isn't: glass responds to what passes behind it, not to the fact that you navigated. A highlight that fires on every transition is a flourish about itself.
Removed
Staggered entrance
Cut. A Material idiom, not an iOS one — iOS lists arrive as one unit. It also delayed content, contradicting this document's own rule that a transition may never gate input.
Removed
Haptics
Scoped down. "Fire on every transition commit" is far more than iOS does — navigation gets no haptic at all. Kept only where there's a real detent: the interruptible-back threshold, and set completion in the logger.
Narrowed
Zoom, honestly
Held, with a caveat now stated plainly: iOS 26's .zoom transition is gesture-driven and interruptible from the first frame. On the zoom specifically, the spring gets it most of the way but the gesture-from-frame-one behaviour needs the native transition, not a re-implementation. Web will be the approximation here; that's an accepted, named gap rather than a claim of parity.
Caveated
The elevated centre FAB
Noted, not changed. A raised centre action in the tab bar is a Material/Instagram idiom rather than an iOS one — Apple tab bars have no elevated slot. It's a deliberate brand decision that already ships, and it isn't this document's to overturn; but it's the reason the nav will never read as fully native, no matter how good the lens is.
Flagged

Guardrails

Rules and risks

  • Reduce Motion means SUBSTITUTE, not delete. The first draft said transitions should “resolve to their end state instantly” and that useMotion() should return zeroed durations. That's wrong, and it's now fixed on this page: Apple's guidance is to swap the positional animation for a cross-dissolve, because a Reduce Motion user still needs to perceive that the screen changed — an instant cut removes the change signal entirely. motion.ts should therefore return a 150 ms cross-dissolve, not zero. Toggle Reduce Motion at the top of this page and replay anything: nothing snaps, everything dissolves.
  • Nothing over 450 ms. The zoom at 440 is the ceiling and earns it by moving a large distance. A 400 ms fade is just slow.
  • A transition may never gate input. Taps during an animation queue to the destination. Stagger in particular must never delay a tappable row past ~150 ms.
  • Don't stack effects. Zoom plus blur plus scale-back plus stagger, all at once, causes motion sickness in a real share of users. One primary motion per transition; everything else supports it quietly.
  • Back is the exact inverse of forward. If it isn't, the spatial model breaks and the motion becomes noise.
  • Parity by construction. Per the project rule every item ships on both clients in the same change — which is why the token file comes first, before anything that consumes it.
  • Watch the JS-driver decision. useEntrance() uses the JS driver deliberately, to avoid a Fabric race that stranded screens invisible. Any rewrite onto Reanimated must be verified on a real device against that exact failure, not assumed fixed.
  • Web has a code-splitting interaction. The shell lazy-loads nearly every screen; one arriving mid-transition will pop unless the transition waits on the chunk. Worth pairing with the planned web-code-splitting work.
